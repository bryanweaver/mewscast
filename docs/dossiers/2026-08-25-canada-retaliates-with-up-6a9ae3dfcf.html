Consensus Facts

  • Canada announced retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods set to take effect September 8, 2026 (CNN, Al Jazeera, AP, ABC News).
  • The retaliatory tariffs will be set at rates of 15%, 25% or 50%, framed by Canada as matching U.S. tariffs 'dollar for dollar' (CNN, Al Jazeera, AP, ABC News).
  • Canada's tariffs target more than 700 U.S. products, including steel, aluminum, fish/seafood, dairy/cheese, appliances and electronics (CNN, AP).
  • The Canadian measures come after the U.S. imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion worth of Canadian goods, which took effect over the weekend (CNN implies via context; explicitly stated by Al Jazeera, AP, ABC News).
  • The U.S. tariffs cover about 5% (5.5% per Al Jazeera) of Canada's exports to the United States (CNN, Al Jazeera, AP).
  • The tariff escalation followed the collapse of trade negotiations in Washington (Al Jazeera, AP, ABC News).
  • Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney accused Washington of trying to subordinate Canada and characterized the U.S. tariffs as an attack (AP articles 4 and 5, Al Jazeera).
  • President Donald Trump said on Monday he would double tariffs on Canadian cars and auto parts to 50%, effective January 1, 2027 (CNN, AP, ABC News).
  • Trump made social-media statements criticizing Canada as difficult/unreasonable and asserting the U.S. does not need Canada (CNN, Al Jazeera, ABC News).
  • Carney said trade talks failed because the U.S. 'asked too much and offered too little' (AP article 5, ABC News).
