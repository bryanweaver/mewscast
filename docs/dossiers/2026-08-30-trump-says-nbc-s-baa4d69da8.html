Consensus Facts

  • On Sunday, Aug. 30, 2026, President Donald Trump said in a Truth Social post that NBC's 'Meet the Press' host Kristen Welker would be reported to the FCC for 'rebuke or punishment.'
  • Trump's post was triggered by Welker describing the results of his endorsed candidates as 'mixed.'
  • Welker made the 'mixed results' comment during a pre-show live shot on NBC's Washington, D.C. affiliate WRC-TV, not during the actual 'Meet the Press' broadcast.
  • In that live shot, Welker said Trump would 'loom large over these midterms' and noted his 'pick of Senator Darline Graham,' sister of the late Lindsey Graham, was successful in her primary battle.
  • Trump called Welker the 'Unpopular Hostess' of the 'once great Meet the Press, now considered Meet the Fake Press' and accused her of 'purposeful inaccuracy.'
  • Trump cited a 100% success rate for Senate endorsements and 98%/99% for House endorsements, and named the wins of Darline Graham and Mike Mazzei.
  • Trump invoked FCC Chair Brendan Carr by name, expressing hope the commission would 'take this Threat to our Country very seriously.'
  • NBC News defended Welker, stating: 'Kristen is one of the best in the business, and we stand by her.'
  • The FCC licenses individual broadcast stations rather than national networks and states its authority over news content accuracy or bias is narrow, and it is prohibited from censorship and infringing First Amendment rights.
  • Some Trump-endorsed candidates lost primaries, including MyPillow founder Mike Lindell, who finished second in Minnesota's Republican gubernatorial primary.
  • NBC News published a report in August 2026 noting Trump's endorsement record took a hit after several endorsed candidates lost primaries.
  • In June 2026, Trump walked out of an hour-long 'Meet the Press' interview with Welker after a tense exchange.
  • Disney/ABC sued the FCC in August 2026, alleging a 'retaliatory campaign' involving early broadcast-license renewal requirements.
