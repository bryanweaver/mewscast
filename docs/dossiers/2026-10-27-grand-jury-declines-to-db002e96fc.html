Consensus Facts

  • A federal grand jury in Washington, D.C., declined to indict former White House aide Cassidy Hutchinson on Tuesday, Oct. 27, 2026.
  • The attempted indictment was tied to Hutchinson's 2022 testimony before the House Select Committee investigating the Jan. 6, 2021, attack on the U.S. Capitol.
  • The Justice Department's Civil Rights Division handled the Hutchinson investigation.
  • DOJ litigator Neville Hedley told U.S. Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui during a hearing that the grand jury had not concurred on the proposed indictment, without naming Hutchinson in open court.
  • Multiple sources separately confirmed the unnamed defendant was Hutchinson.
  • Hutchinson was a senior aide to then-White House chief of staff Mark Meadows.
  • Hutchinson testified that Tony Ornato told her Trump lunged for the steering wheel of his limo and reacted angrily after a Secret Service agent refused to drive him to the Capitol following his Ellipse speech.
  • The Washington Post first reported that the grand jury declined to indict Hutchinson.
  • The failed indictment is described as one of several setbacks for federal prosecutors during the second Trump administration.
  • Trump has long accused Hutchinson of lying, while Hutchinson has maintained her testimony was truthful.
  • The DOJ did not immediately respond to requests for comment; attorneys for Hutchinson also did not immediately respond.
