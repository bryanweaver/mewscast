Consensus Facts

  • The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) won a strong first-place finish in the Saxony-Anhalt state election on Sunday, September 6, 2026, according to exit polls.
  • Exit polls for ARD and ZDF public broadcasters put the AfD at around 44-44.5% of the vote, more than double its result five years earlier.
  • Chancellor Friedrich Merz's center-right Christian Democratic Union (CDU) came second at around 18.5%, roughly half its previous support; the CDU has led the state for about 24 years.
  • It was unclear whether the AfD could form a government, as exit polls put it at least marginally short of an absolute majority.
  • Ulrich Siegmund, the AfD's 35-year-old lead candidate for state governor, said 'we have written/made history' and told ARD that voters had signaled 'things cannot carry on like this.'
  • AfD co-leader Alice Weidel called the result 'historic' and said the party had a clear mandate to govern.
  • A far-right or right-wing populist party has not led a German state, national, or major-city government since World War II; this would be a first if the AfD takes power.
  • The AfD in Saxony-Anhalt has been classified as a confirmed right-wing extremist organization by Germany's domestic intelligence agency, a designation the party rejects.
  • Mainstream parties maintain a 'firewall' policy refusing to form coalitions or cooperate with the AfD, and CDU officials reaffirmed that stance after the result.
  • The AfD's platform includes hardline anti-immigration measures such as deportations and 'remigration,' and Saxony-Anhalt is located in Germany's less prosperous former-communist east (population about 2.1 million).
  • The Left Party polled around 9% and the center-left SPD and Greens each cleared the threshold at roughly 8-9%; it was uncertain whether the BSW party would reach the 5% threshold.
  • The result is described as a blow to Merz and his national coalition government amid discontent over a sluggish economy.
