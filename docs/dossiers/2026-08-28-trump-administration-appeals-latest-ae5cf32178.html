Consensus Facts

  • A federal judge in Boston, U.S. District Court Judge Indira Talwani (an Obama nominee), blocked implementation of Trump's executive order limiting mail voting for two weeks.
  • The ruling came Thursday (Aug. 27, 2026), after Democrats and voting rights groups refiled lawsuits to comply with a recent Supreme Court decision.
  • The Supreme Court's conservative majority earlier this week issued a procedural decision allowing the administration to move ahead, saying Talwani had acted too soon, without ruling on the order's legality.
  • Under the order, the U.S. Postal Service could refuse to deliver mail ballots from states that do not submit lists of eligible voters and follow a uniform/specified envelope format.
  • Talwani wrote that plaintiff states have neither the time nor funds to design new ballots, seek design approval, order production, update election management systems, train officials on the USPS portal, and upload voter data before the midterms.
  • Nearly one-third of Americans vote by mail, and election officials contend there isn't enough time to revise systems to comply with the new Postal Service directives.
  • Trump has long targeted mail voting, which he falsely blames for his 2020 election loss even though he uses it to cast his own ballot.
  • A 2025 Brookings Institution report found mail voting fraud occurred in only about four cases out of every 10 million mail ballots cast.
  • Trump issued the mail-voting executive order in March 2026, and opponents challenged it in court shortly after.
  • The case could be appealed back to the Supreme Court and involves the U.S. 1st Circuit Court of Appeals.
