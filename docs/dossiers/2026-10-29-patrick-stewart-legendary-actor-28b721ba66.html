Consensus Facts

  • Patrick Stewart, the British actor, died at age 86.
  • He died on Wednesday at his home in Los Angeles.
  • His death followed 'a brief and poignant journey' with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia, per a statement from spokesperson Kelly Bush Novak.
  • He was best known for playing Captain Jean-Luc Picard in the 'Star Trek' franchise and Professor Charles Xavier in the 'X-Men' franchise.
  • He played Picard across 'Star Trek: The Next Generation' (seven seasons), four feature films, and three seasons of the Paramount+ series 'Star Trek: Picard.'
  • He was set to reprise the role of Professor X in 'Avengers: Doomsday,' scheduled for release in December 2026.
  • He joined the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1966 and had a decades-long classical stage career on Broadway and London's West End.
  • He was born in Mirfield/Yorkshire, England, in 1940.
  • He is survived by his wife Sunny (Ozell) and two children, Daniel and Sophie/Sophia.
  • He was nominated for a Tony Award in 2008 for his Broadway performance in 'Macbeth.'
  • Marvel Studios released a statement calling him a 'singular talent,' 'generous collaborator,' and 'true gentleman' who defined Xavier for generations of fans.
  • 'X-Men' co-star Hugh Jackman paid tribute, calling him 'one of the greats of stage and screen' and 'a tremendous friend and man.'
  • 'Star Trek' co-star William Shatner paid tribute, saying Stewart brought 'intelligence, grace, and a deep sense of humanity to everything he did.'
  • His spokesperson's statement said he was 'held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter' and praised his 'invincible grace.'
