Consensus Facts

  • Federal prosecutors in U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro's office moved to drop charges against defendants accused of vandalizing the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool in Washington, D.C.
  • Former Olympic canoeist David Hearn had his felony vandalism charge dropped on Friday (filed July 31); prosecutors cited new information that the damage resulted from a 'botched' or 'flawed' contractor installation rushed to finish ahead of America 250 celebrations.
  • Three additional defendants — Justin Carreno, Sophie Dennison-Gibby, and Cameron Thiers — had misdemeanor charges dropped; they had been accused of destroying property valued at less than $1,000.
  • Prosecutors' court filing stated the Department of the Interior provided incomplete information and that damage was the result of a flawed installation by the contractor, not vandalism.
  • President Trump publicly disagreed '100%' with the decision, insisting on Truth Social that 'major damage was caused by VANDALS' while conceding there may have been 'some contractor difficulty.'
  • On Monday (Aug. 3), Trump told reporters in the Oval Office that Pirro 'choked' and 'folded like an umbrella,' saying he was 'really disappointed' with her.
  • Hearn was arrested June 19 after reaching down to touch the pool during a bike ride and denied any wrongdoing; his lawyers said the case 'should never have been brought' and that the government owes him an apology.
  • The Reflecting Pool renovation/resealing project cost roughly $16 million and was part of Trump's effort to beautify the capital ahead of the July 4, 2026 America 250 anniversary; the pool later suffered peeling sealant and algae and was drained again.
  • Attorneys for the dismissed defendants described the experience as a 'nightmare' and 'humiliating'; Carreno's attorney said his client was fired from his government contractor job and had to move homes, and said the government 'literally fabricated a crime.'
