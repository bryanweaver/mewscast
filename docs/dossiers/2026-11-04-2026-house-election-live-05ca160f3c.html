Consensus Facts

  • US media outlets project that Democrats will win control of the US House of Representatives in the 2026 midterm elections.
  • Democrats needed a net gain of three seats to secure the House majority of 218.
  • In North Carolina, Democrat Jamie Ager defeated Republican state Rep. Jennifer Balkcom (also rendered Capps Balkcom) in a district Trump carried by more than 9 points in 2024; Balkcom replaced Rep. Chuck Edwards, who dropped his reelection bid amid ethics/harassment matters.
  • Democrats flipped multiple GOP-held House seats, including Republican incumbents in Pennsylvania (Rob Bresnahan lost to Paige Cognetti; Ryan Mackenzie and Scott Perry also lost) and Colorado (Gabe Evans lost to Manny Rutinel).
  • The election was widely framed as a referendum on President Trump's second term, with voters citing cost of living/economic discontent and government corruption as top issues.
  • Voter dissatisfaction was tied to the ongoing Iran war and Trump's tariff policies, which were linked to rising prices on gas and groceries.
  • If Democrats take the House, they will gain subpoena power and plan aggressive oversight investigations into Trump's administration, family, and business ventures, with Reps. Robert Garcia (Oversight) and others leading.
  • Historically the sitting president's party loses House seats in midterms; outlets cite only rare exceptions.
  • Republicans pursued mid-decade redistricting in states including Texas and Florida to protect their majority, but the effort had limited impact; Democrats countered with redistricting in California.
  • House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries has laid out priorities including reversing Medicaid cuts, extending Affordable Care Act credits, rolling back tariffs, and ending the Iran war; he has said Democrats 'haven't ruled anything in or out' on a third Trump impeachment.
  • Trump distanced himself from the losses, posting that he was not on the ballot and would have won 'in a landslide' if he were.
