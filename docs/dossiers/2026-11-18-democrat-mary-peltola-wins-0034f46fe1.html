Consensus Facts

  • Democrat Mary Peltola defeated incumbent Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan in Alaska's US Senate race.
  • Peltola reached 50.1% of the vote, clearing the majority threshold so the race did not go to ranked-choice tabulation.
  • The win gives Democrats a 56-44 Senate majority beginning in January 2026.
  • Democrats flipped nine Republican-held Senate seats this cycle as part of a midterm wave.
  • Alaska was the last Senate race to be called after the November 3 election.
  • A second candidate named Dan J. Sullivan (a retired teacher/school teacher) entered the race, prompting Republican accusations that Democrats recruited him to confuse voters.
  • The New York Times reported that Democratic operatives linked to Peltola's campaign tried to recruit the spoiler candidate; Peltola denied involvement and Sullivan Jr. denied links to her campaign.
  • The Trump administration/Justice Department filed a lawsuit to block certification if the incumbent Sullivan lost, which a federal judge dismissed in early November; the DOJ filed a notice to appeal.
  • Peltola is poised to become the first Native American senator from Alaska, sharing the distinction of first Native American women senators with Peggy Flanagan.
  • Peltola previously held Alaska's at-large House seat (elected in 2022) before losing reelection in 2024.
  • Peltola issued a statement saying 'the status quo in DC is not working for Alaska' and vowed to put Alaska first.
  • Alaska GOP Gov. Mike Dunleavy ordered/launched an investigation into Peltola's campaign over the spoiler-candidate allegations.
