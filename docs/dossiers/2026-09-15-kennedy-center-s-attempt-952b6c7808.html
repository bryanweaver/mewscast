Consensus Facts

  • On Tuesday, Sept. 15, 2026, U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper blocked the Kennedy Center's latest attempt to add President Donald Trump's name to the building.
  • Cooper wrote: 'Simply put, Defendants cannot install memorials for President Trump or anyone or anything else at the Kennedy Center without Congress's blessing,' and that the board resolution 'bucks a federal court order and a statute Congress enacted.'
  • The blocked measure would have added an inscription reading that the center was 'renovated and restored by' (or 'Restored and Renovated by President') Donald J. Trump on the building's facade and renamed the surrounding grounds the 'President Donald J. Trump Plaza.'
  • The board approved this resolution on Aug. 13, 2026.
  • The ruling stems from a lawsuit brought by Rep. Joyce Beatty (D-Ohio), a Kennedy Center board/trustee member who previously secured Cooper's earlier order.
  • In May 2026, Cooper ordered Trump's name removed from the center, ruling only Congress could rename the institution created by a 1964 federal law honoring President John F. Kennedy.
  • Cooper declined to rule on a separate proposed inscription tied to 'The Trump Kennedy Center Fund' / '$100 million' fundraising threshold, finding that issue premature.
  • The Kennedy Center board, now stacked with Trump allies, was set to meet Tuesday, and the board still planned to vote on closing the building for renovations.
  • The proposals to add Trump's name were removed/shelved from Tuesday's board meeting agenda, with the board to focus on the closure question.
  • Board materials argued Trump was unlikely to lead the 'fiscal rescue' of the center or oversee the renovation without 'appropriate recognition' on the building.
  • Cooper's ruling was issued in a 22-page opinion/order.
