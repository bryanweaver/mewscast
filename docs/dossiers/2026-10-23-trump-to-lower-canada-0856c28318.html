Consensus Facts

  • Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine) announced on Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, that the Trump administration would grant tariff exemptions on some Canadian imports used by Maine's agriculture industry (reported by NBC News and Politico).
  • The exemptions cover 50% duties on certain products, including items relevant to Maine's blueberry, potato, apple, and seaweed producers, plus packing materials, refrigeration/freezing equipment and other specialized goods (NBC News and Politico).
  • The overall amount of tariffs on Canadian goods will remain unchanged; the duties are being shifted to other imported product categories rather than eliminated (NBC News references added tariffs on 'certain valves, carpets, waste products'; Politico cites roughly 100 new categories including cocoa powder, shampoos, carpets, canoes and wooden coffins).
  • The action was confirmed by a White House document/executive order released Friday afternoon (NBC News and Politico).
  • Collins is in a tight re-election race against Democrat Troy Jackson, and the move is framed as a boost for her ahead of the Nov. 3 midterm election (NBC News and Politico; corroborated in detail by CNBC).
