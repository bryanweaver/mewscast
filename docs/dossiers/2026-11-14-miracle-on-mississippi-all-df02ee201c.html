Consensus Facts

  • A tanker identified as the Atlantic Asphalt collided with the river cruise paddleboat American Heritage while it was docked on the Mississippi River near the Port of New Orleans on Friday, Nov. 13, 2026.
  • The collision occurred at approximately 12:30 p.m. local time, roughly two hours after the American Heritage had docked for passenger disembarkation (ABC News, CBS News).
  • All passengers and crew aboard both vessels were accounted for, with no fatalities reported.
  • New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno called the outcome 'a miracle on the Mississippi.'
  • 32 people were taken to hospitals with non-critical injuries, including at least two police officers who were in the water during the rescue (AP, ABC, BBC, LA Times, CBS).
  • After the collision the cruise ship took on water, began to sink, listed, and eventually toppled over, sending people into the Mississippi River.
  • The rescue involved tugboat operators, nearby workers, police, Coast Guard, and private vessels/volunteers who happened to be on the river.
  • U.S. Coast Guard Capt. Melanie Burnham said the tanker appeared to have suffered 'some type of loss of steering,' with the exact cause still unknown and under investigation.
  • The American Heritage was built in 2015, is operated by American Cruise Lines, is five stories tall, and can hold up to 150 passengers; it was finishing an eight-day cruise.
  • The National Transportation Safety Board planned to investigate, but was affected by a nationwide ground stop that disrupted flights, forcing its team to drive rather than fly (AP, CBS).
  • The river water temperature was 66 degrees Fahrenheit (19 degrees Celsius) (AP, LA Times).
