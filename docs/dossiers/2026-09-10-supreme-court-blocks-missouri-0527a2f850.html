Consensus Facts

  • On Thursday, Sept. 10, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court blocked Missouri from using a newly drawn Republican-favoring congressional map for the November 2026 midterm elections.
  • The Supreme Court's order was unsigned/brief with no noted dissents, and the justices gave no reasoning for their decision.
  • The ruling blocked an order by U.S. District Judge Stephen Clark, a Trump appointee, who ruled Tuesday that the new map must be used.
  • Missouri's current U.S. House delegation has six Republicans and two Democrats; the new map would likely have shifted this to a 7-1 Republican split.
  • The new map targeted the seat of longtime Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver.
  • The state is now expected to use the previous 2022 congressional map.
  • Missouri Secretary of State Denny Hoskins, a Republican, led the effort to use the new map.
  • This was the second time the fight reached the Supreme Court; earlier in the week Justice Brett Kavanaugh, who handles emergency matters from Missouri, rejected a request from Hoskins to block the Missouri Supreme Court ruling.
  • The Missouri Supreme Court ruled in favor of challengers (the group People Not Politicians, led by Richard von Glahn) and ordered use of the existing districts.
  • The Missouri Supreme Court held a contempt hearing Thursday regarding Hoskins defying its order, which convened around the same time as the Supreme Court's ruling.
  • The new map was enacted by Missouri Republicans (in 2025) as part of a nationwide redistricting push led by President Trump to hold the GOP House majority.
  • Hoskins' lawyers argued minutes before the ruling that changing maps would risk the disenfranchisement of hundreds of thousands of voters who participated in the primary using the new map.
  • First/mail ballots in Missouri are set to go out around Sept. 19.
  • The state's primaries were held (in August) using the new, more GOP-friendly maps.
