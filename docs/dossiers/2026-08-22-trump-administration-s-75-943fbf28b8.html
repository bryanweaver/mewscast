Consensus Facts

  • A federal judge struck down the Trump administration's ban on issuing immigrant visas to people from 75 countries, ruling it was 'contrary to law.'
  • The ruling was issued Friday, August 22, 2026, by District Judge Jeannette A. Vargas of the federal court in Manhattan (Southern District of New York).
  • The judge found the policy exceeded the statutory authority of Secretary of State Marco Rubio.
  • The State Department justified the ban by claiming immigrants from the targeted countries were likely to need public assistance or 'take welfare and public benefits.'
  • The policy barred immigrant visas even where consular officers individually determined an applicant could be self-sufficient.
  • A diplomatic cable from Rubio to consular posts, entered into the case record, directed officers to refuse applicants even when they provided evidence overcoming the public-charge concern.
  • The ban applied to immigrant (permanent-residence) visas for family- and employment-based applicants, not to nonimmigrant visas such as tourist or student visas.
  • Plaintiffs included six U.S. citizens who filed family-based petitions and applicants who filed employment-based (job-based) petitions.
  • The ruling vacated the ban and set aside visa denials that were based solely on the policy.
  • Countries affected included Afghanistan, Brazil, Yemen, and dozens of others; the ban covered roughly 40% of the world's nations.
  • The judge distinguished the case from the 2018 Supreme Court decision upholding Trump's first-term travel ban, noting that case concerned entry while this case concerned visa issuance.
