Consensus Facts

  • Paraguay's President Santiago Peña was impeached and removed from office on Friday by the Senate and placed under house arrest.
  • The crisis was triggered by the collapse of Ueno Bank, a major digital bank seized by Paraguay's central bank earlier in the week.
  • Ueno Bank had roughly 3 million customers, a large share of Paraguay's adult population, who lost access to their savings and bank cards.
  • Peña, a former IMF economist, had held a stake in the parent/holding company linked to Ueno Bank until last year (March 2025 per Al Jazeera).
  • Peña appointed a former Ueno-connected executive, Carlos Carvallo, to head the central bank; Carvallo has since resigned.
  • Vice-President Pedro Alliana, a member of the conservative Colorado Party, was sworn in as interim president and will serve until August 2028.
  • Impeachment charges centered on conflicts of interest, influence over regulators, and the diversion/deposit of public pension funds into Ueno (BBC and Hindustan Times cite the pension fund figure; Hindustan Times and Al Jazeera cite the $355m/$380m pension deposit).
  • Peña offered to resign/step down before the vote but senators proceeded with impeachment; he denied any wrongdoing.
  • Prosecutors ordered Peña to remain under house arrest pending questioning by anti-corruption/economic crimes authorities.
  • The central bank said deposits were insured up to roughly $36,000-$37,000, covering nearly all customers.

Hindustan Times: The state pension fund deposited $355 million, allegedly exceeding legal limits.
Date the central bank seized the bank
Al Jazeera English: Central bank put Ueno into resolution on Monday.

Deposit insurance ceiling
Al Jazeera English: Fund insures up to about $36,000 per customer, covering ~97 percent of customers.
Hindustan Times: Deposits insured up to approximately $37,000, covering nearly all Ueno customers.
Status of related prosecutions
Hindustan Times: Prosecutors charged 27 people including controlling shareholder Miguel Vázquez; six bank directors in pretrial detention; arrest of ex-central bank chief Carvallo ordered; luxury assets seized.
BBC News: Does not report the 27 charges or asset seizures; focuses on Peña's impeachment and upcoming questioning.
Al Jazeera English: Notes only that the Public Prosecutor's office ordered multiple criminal investigations, without the 27-person figure.
