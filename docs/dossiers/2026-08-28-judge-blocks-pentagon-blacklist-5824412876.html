Consensus Facts

  • U.S. District Judge Rita Lin, a Biden appointee in the Northern District of California, ruled on Thursday, August 27, 2026, that the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a 'supply chain risk' was unlawful.
  • The court found the Department of Defense violated Anthropic's First Amendment rights by retaliating against the company for its public criticism of the government's views on AI.
  • Lin wrote that 'the empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics.'
  • The dispute arose after Anthropic refused to allow the military to use its Claude AI models for autonomous weapons or domestic/mass surveillance, arguing its models were not reliable enough for such uses and that domestic surveillance violates rights.
  • Anthropic was the first U.S. company to be publicly designated a supply chain risk under a government procurement statute previously used only for companies tied to foreign adversaries.
  • The designation barred the Pentagon and its defense contractors from using Anthropic's products/technology.
  • Anthropic filed suit in March (its complaint dated March 9, 2026) alleging First Amendment (free speech) and Fifth Amendment (due process) violations.
  • Anthropic has a second, separate lawsuit still pending in Washington, D.C. over a distinct supply chain risk designation.
  • An Anthropic spokesperson said the company welcomes the ruling and remains focused on working productively with the government to harness AI for national security.
  • Anthropic executives said the designation could cost the company billions of dollars in lost business and reputational harm.
  • The government/DOJ argued the designation stemmed from Anthropic's refusal to accept contractual terms rather than its views on AI safety, and that Anthropic's refusal to lift restrictions risked disabling military systems during operations.
