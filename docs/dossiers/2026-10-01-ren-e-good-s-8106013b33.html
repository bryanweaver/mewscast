Consensus Facts

  • Renee Good, a 37-year-old mother of three, was fatally shot by an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer in Minneapolis on January 7, 2026.
  • On Thursday, October 1, 2026, Good's family filed two civil lawsuits over her death.
  • The lawsuits were filed by Good's partner, Becca Good, and her brother, Brent Ganger.
  • The ICE officer who shot Good is identified in court filings as Jonathan Ross (also spelled Jonathon Ross by Al Jazeera).
  • One lawsuit targets the U.S. government for wrongful death and related claims; the second names Ross and senior Trump administration officials.
  • Named defendants in the second lawsuit include White House deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller and former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem.
  • The shooting occurred during a federal immigration enforcement operation in Minneapolis dubbed Operation Metro Surge.
  • Good was shot in the driver's seat of her SUV; bystander cellphone video captured the encounter.
  • Federal officials at the time said the officer acted in self-defense and claimed Good had engaged in 'an act of domestic terrorism.'
  • Becca Good spoke publicly about her partner's death for the first time at the press conference announcing the lawsuits.
  • The White House, through spokeswoman Lauren Bis, said it would 'vigorously defend' the lawsuits.
  • President Trump previously commented on the shooting, referencing 'a couple versions of that tape that are very, very bad' and the way Good's car 'was pulled away.'
  • The lawsuits seek damages for wrongful death and allege excessive force; the suit against officials frames Operation Metro Surge as a conspiracy to violate civil rights.
  • A second U.S. citizen, Alex Pretti, was also killed during the Minneapolis crackdown weeks after Good.
