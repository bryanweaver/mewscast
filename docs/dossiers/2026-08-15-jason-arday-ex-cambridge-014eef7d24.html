Consensus Facts

  • Jason Arday, a former University of Cambridge professor, was found dead at a south London address on Friday.
  • Arday was 41 years old.
  • He was found unresponsive and pronounced dead at the scene; the Metropolitan Police said the death is being treated as unexpected but is not believed to be suspicious.
  • Arday resigned from his Cambridge professorship (sociology of education) in early August 2026 amid plagiarism allegations and questions about his accomplishments.
  • In his resignation statement, Arday said the scrutiny he faced went 'beyond scholarly disagreement' and that the personal cost had become too great.
  • Arday was celebrated as the youngest Black professor at Cambridge, hired in 2023.
  • Plagiarism allegations were first raised publicly by Nathan Cofnas, a former Cambridge academic who had left/been removed after criticizing diversity, equity and inclusion programs.
  • Cambridge Vice-Chancellor Deborah Prentice said the university was 'desperately saddened' and offered sympathies to Arday's family.
  • Arday's family released a statement calling him a gentle man who wanted to see the best in everyone and describing a 'campaign of misinformation' against him.
  • Cambridge initially defended Arday, citing that Liverpool John Moores University (which awarded his 2015 PhD) had investigated plagiarism allegations, before later opening its own independent investigation into his appointment and qualifications.
  • Arday's memoir, 'Great and Unfortunate Things,' published by Simon & Schuster, was released the same week (August 11).
  • The Good Law Project's Jo/Jolyon Maugham questioned the public interest in continuing to 'hound' Arday after his resignation and what risk assessments media outlets performed.
