Consensus Facts

  • The Senate voted 51-44 on Wednesday, Aug. 5, 2026, to confirm Dr. Erica Schwartz as director of the CDC (NBC News, Axios, ABC News, CNN).
  • The vote fell largely along party lines, with Sen. Tim Kaine (D-Va.) the only Democrat to support Schwartz (NBC News, Axios, CNN).
  • Schwartz becomes the CDC's first permanent director in roughly a year (NBC News, Axios, ABC News, CNN, Pharmacy Times).
  • Schwartz previously served as deputy surgeon general during Trump's first administration and was a Navy physician and Coast Guard chief medical officer (NBC News, Axios, ABC News, CNN, Pharmacy Times).
  • President Trump nominated Schwartz in April 2026, one month past the legal deadline for naming a permanent director (NBC News, ABC News, Pharmacy Times).
  • A series of acting directors led the CDC since August 2025, when Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. fired previous director Susan Monarez after 29 days (about less than a month) (NBC News, Axios, CNN, Pharmacy Times).
  • Monarez said she was dismissed for refusing to rubber-stamp Kennedy's vaccine policies (NBC News, Axios, CNN).
  • Schwartz will replace/succeed Dr. Jay Bhattacharya, who ran the CDC while also directing the NIH since February (NBC News, ABC News, CNN).
  • At her July confirmation hearing, Schwartz faced tough questioning about her independence from Kennedy and vaccine policy, and public health experts said she failed to give clear answers (NBC News, Axios, ABC News).
  • Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-La.), HELP Committee chair and a physician, was initially skeptical but decided to support Schwartz after conversations, saying 'she knows what she is doing and will stand against those who do not' (NBC News, Axios, CNN).
  • Cassidy's support was tied in part to HHS modifying a CDC webpage about vaccines and autism (NBC News, CNN); the disputed claim was edited but discussions were described as ongoing.
  • Schwartz will inherit a measles crisis at a 35-year high and a growing cyclosporiasis outbreak linked to shredded iceberg lettuce (NBC News, Pharmacy Times).

Disagreements

Interim leadership sequence after Monarez's firing
NBC News: A series of acting directors led since August 2025; Schwartz takes over from Jay Bhattacharya, who has helmed the agency since February while also directing NIH.
CNN: Kennedy installed then-deputy secretary Jim O'Neill as interim head first; officials later soured on O'Neill, and the agency was then overseen by Bhattacharya.
ABC News: Schwartz will replace Bhattacharya, who took over as acting CDC director in February.
Schwartz's stance on the vaccine-autism webpage at the hearing
NBC News: Schwartz said vaccines are safe and effective but was unaware of some of Kennedy's vaccine policy changes and dodged questions.
ABC News: Sen. Bernie Sanders asked if she would remove the webpage; Schwartz accepted there is 'overwhelming evidence' vaccines don't cause autism but did not commit to removing the webpage.
Axios: She hedged on whether she would tell Congress if told to adopt 'unscientific' or harmful policies, saying she didn't believe Trump or Kennedy would do that.
Framing of who nominated/described Schwartz
ABC News: Trump nominated her in April, describing her as 'incredibly talented.'
CNN: Describes her as a notable departure from prior MAHA-line contenders and the first Black woman to serve as CDC director.
Pharmacy Times: Nominated April 16, 2026; emphasizes her 27 years of uniformed service and MD/MPH/JD credentials.
