Consensus Facts

  • A former Cornell University student known as Jane Doe alleges she was sexually assaulted by members of the Chi Phi fraternity at a fraternity house in October 2024.
  • Jane Doe recently filed a civil lawsuit against Cornell University and seven then-Chi Phi fraternity members.
  • Cornell conducted an internal investigation; two students were expelled and two were suspended, with additional lesser sanctions for others.
  • No criminal charges were brought against the accused at the time of the original 2024 probe.
  • New York Gov. Kathy Hochul appointed New York Attorney General Letitia James to serve as special prosecutor/oversee the case (CBS dates the executive order Oct. 1; NBC says James replaced DA Van Houten the prior week).
  • Cornell students staged a seven-hour sit-in on Oct. 7, 2026, in solidarity with Jane Doe (NBC and CNN); NBC reports roughly 80 students gathered at the Toni Morrison dining hall.
  • Cornell faculty introduced/are reviewing a resolution calling for a no-confidence vote in the university administration over its handling of the case (NBC; CNN reports it will be reviewed for the Oct. 14 Faculty Senate agenda).
  • Cornell brought in a former top U.S. Justice Department official — identified by ABC as former Deputy Attorney General Sally Yates — to lead an independent review of the university's handling of the allegations.
  • Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten declined to charge the accused and has been criticized for that decision; the DA and university defended their original 2024 probes.
  • The alleged assault is described as having occurred over multiple hours during the night of Oct. 19-20, 2024, with Jane Doe alleging she was given ketamine (NBC and CBS).
