Consensus Facts

  • A high-speed train operated by state-owned RENFE struck the rear of a stopped high-speed train operated by private company IRYO in Toledo province, central Spain, on Wednesday, Oct. 28, 2026.
  • The collision occurred on the Madrid–Seville (Madrid–Andalusia) high-speed line, near the town of Urda, roughly 118–150 km south of Madrid.
  • At least 68 people were killed, according to authorities (the AP, Reuters, CNBC and Reuters graphics all cite this figure).
  • Dozens were injured, with figures reported around 50 to nearly 80 across outlets.
  • The crash is Spain's second deadly high-speed rail collision of 2026, following a January crash on the same route/system.
  • Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez traveled to or addressed the scene, said 'all of Spain is grieving,' and said any negligence would come to light; he declared three days of national mourning.
  • Transport Minister Óscar Puente said a computer/communications security system failure automatically stopped trains, and that an 'inexplicable'/erroneous decision to restart the RENFE train despite an active stop (red) signal caused the crash.
  • High-speed rail service between Madrid and Andalusia was suspended following the crash.
  • Spain faces a snap national election on Nov. 29, 2026, which Sánchez called amid unrest over the housing crisis.
  • The crash is Spain's deadliest rail disaster since the 2013 derailment.
