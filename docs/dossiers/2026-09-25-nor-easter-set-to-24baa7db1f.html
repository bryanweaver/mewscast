Consensus Facts

  • A nor'easter is affecting the US East Coast, forecast to continue through the weekend of Sept. 25-28, 2026 (ABC, USA Today, NBC, CBS, Guardian).
  • The storm is unusual for occurring so early in the season, in September (NBC, Guardian).
  • Coastal flooding is a primary threat, driven by prolonged onshore northeast winds and worsened by a full moon Saturday night raising tides (ABC, USA Today, NBC, CBS, Guardian).
  • Significant beach erosion is expected from the pounding surf along the coast (ABC, USA Today, NBC, CBS, Guardian).
  • Strong wind gusts, generally in the 40-60 mph range along the coast, are forecast, with risk of downed trees and power lines and resulting power outages (ABC, USA Today, NBC, CBS, Guardian).
  • Southeastern Virginia (Virginia Beach/Norfolk area) is identified as being at high risk for major coastal flooding (USA Today, NBC).
  • The storm is expected to disrupt air travel, with flight cancellations and delays (ABC, USA Today, Guardian).
  • Some airlines issued weather waivers for affected East Coast travelers (USA Today references American, JetBlue, Southwest, United; Guardian notes travelers warned of cancellations).
  • Large waves are forecast, ranging roughly from 10-12 feet in the Mid-Atlantic to as high as 17-22 feet in New England (ABC, NBC, USA Today, news.google.com/NBC).
  • Nor'easters are named for their northeasterly winds and are most frequent and violent between September and April, per NOAA (ABC, NBC).
  • Simultaneously, Hurricane Nolo strengthened to a Category 1 storm near Hawaii's Big Island, threatening heavy rain and flooding (NBC, Guardian, news.google.com/NBC).
  • Tens of millions of people are under alert/coastal flood advisories along the East Coast (Guardian cites up to 40 million; news.google.com/NBC cites 33 million under coastal flood alerts).
