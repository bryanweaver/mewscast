Consensus Facts

  • An ICE officer named Christian Castro has been federally charged with making false statements to investigators about a shooting during immigration enforcement operations in Minneapolis, Minnesota.
  • The victim was Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis, a Venezuelan man/immigrant who was shot in the leg and survived (a nonfatal shooting).
  • The shooting occurred on January 14, 2026, during the Minneapolis immigration crackdown.
  • Castro is separately facing state-level charges in Minnesota of assault and false reporting of a crime related to the same shooting.
  • Federal authorities initially accused Sosa-Celis and another man of beating an officer with a broom handle and a snow shovel, but federal prosecutors dropped those charges after video/surveillance evidence showed officers may have lied about the encounter.
  • Castro was part of Operation Metro Surge, a large immigration enforcement operation in the Twin Cities that began in January 2026.
  • Two U.S. citizens were killed by federal officers during the Minneapolis operation, drawing condemnation.
  • Castro was arrested in May in the Minnesota case and held in custody in Brownsville, Texas.
  • Texas Gov. Greg Abbott refused to immediately sign an extradition warrant to hand Castro over to Minnesota, and Castro was released from Texas custody about a week before the federal charges.
  • Castro is the first federal immigration officer to be prosecuted related to actions taken during Operation Metro Surge, described as a rare instance of the Justice Department seeking to hold immigration officers accountable.
  • Sosa-Celis's attorney, Robin Wolpert, criticized the federal charges as 'grossly undercharg[ing]' the case, stating Castro shot her client through the door of an occupied house without a self-defense justification.
  • A separate investigation into whether Castro should face a more serious civil rights charge in the shooting is ongoing.
  • The criminal case was confirmed by a person familiar with the matter who was not authorized to speak publicly; the case was under seal.
  • Federal prosecutor Matthew Evans, who worked on the case, had told lawyers for Sosa-Celis that DOJ officials blocked efforts to bring the more serious civil rights charge, as reported by ProPublica; Evans was subsequently fired and his DOJ email indicated he was on leave.
