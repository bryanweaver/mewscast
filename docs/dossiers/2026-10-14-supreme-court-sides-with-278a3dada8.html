Consensus Facts

  • On Wednesday, Oct. 14, 2026, the Supreme Court sided with the Trump administration and Republicans in a dispute over discounted broadcast campaign ad rates, issuing an unsigned order.
  • Justices Sonia Sotomayor and Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented and would have denied the administration's/appeal applications.
  • The dispute centers on an FCC policy (outlined in a March notice from the FCC's Media Bureau) extending discounted broadcast ad rates — previously reserved for legally qualified candidates — to political parties and joint fundraising committees.
  • Four Democratic candidates for federal office challenged the policy, arguing the discounts should be limited to candidates.
  • The U.S. 4th Circuit Court of Appeals ruled against the FCC/for the Democrats, and later ordered the FCC to resolve the challenge; the FCC/Solicitor General D. John Sauer sought emergency relief from the Supreme Court.
  • Chief Justice John Roberts issued a temporary administrative stay (Oct. 8) blocking the lower court's deadline while the justices considered the request.
  • The Supreme Court wrote that 'our system of separation of powers does not allow the Judiciary to meddle' in the workings of a coordinate branch of government.
  • The ruling is seen as advantaging Republicans, who have more money available through party committees than Democrats.
  • This was the second time the Supreme Court intervened on the Republicans' side in this dispute (after an earlier September order).
  • Democratic FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez objected to the rule change, criticizing the lack of public comment and the process.
