Consensus Facts

  • The University of Michigan announced plans to mask first-semester grades for freshmen in its College of Literature, Science, and the Arts.
  • Under the plan, first-semester classes will appear on official transcripts only as 'P' (pass) or 'NC'/'no credit' rather than letter grades, and those grades will not factor into GPA.
  • The university framed the change as a way to reduce stress and help address the mental health crisis among college-aged students.
  • Both The Washington Post and The Independent report that MIT has previously used a form of pass/no-credit grading for first-year students.
  • The announcement triggered a divided reaction, with support and criticism appearing on social media and among observers.

Disagreements

Effective date of the policy
The Washington Post: Says the plan starts fall 2027 ('starting fall 2027').
The Independent: Says the plan begins 'next fall' and describes it as a pilot program.
Precedent institutions cited
The Washington Post: Cites MIT (pass/no credit since 1968) and Swarthmore College (stopped publishing first-semester letter grades in the 1970s).
The Independent: Cites MIT and the California Institute of Technology (Caltech); does not mention Swarthmore.
Scope / who is affected
The Washington Post: States the LSA college is affected but does not quantify student numbers.
The Independent: Specifies the LSA college educates roughly half of the university's 8,000 first-year undergraduates.
