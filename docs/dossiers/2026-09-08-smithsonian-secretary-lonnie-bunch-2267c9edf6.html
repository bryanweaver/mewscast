Consensus Facts

  • Lonnie G. Bunch III, secretary of the Smithsonian Institution, announced on Tuesday that he will step down/retire by the end of the year.
  • Bunch is the first Black person to lead the Smithsonian and previously founded the National Museum of African American History and Culture.
  • Bunch's departure comes amid the Trump administration's escalating campaign to influence how the Smithsonian presents American history.
  • The Trump administration sent a letter (dated Aug. 28 per CNN) warning that federal agencies may stop working with the Smithsonian if its leadership does not change, citing a report accusing the institution of extreme political activism.
  • Chief Justice John Roberts, who serves as chancellor of the Smithsonian, praised Bunch and said the Regents look forward to working with him on a smooth transition to a successor.
  • In his statement, Bunch said he announces his retirement 'with very mixed emotions and heartfelt gratitude' and leaves 'with pride and sadness, gratitude and joy.'
  • Bunch is 73 years old and has worked at the Smithsonian for nearly 38 years, becoming secretary in 2019 (roughly seven years in the top job).
  • The Smithsonian's Board of Regents will select/name the next secretary and launch a search for a successor.
