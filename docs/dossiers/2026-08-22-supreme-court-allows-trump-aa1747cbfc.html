Consensus Facts

  • On Friday, August 21, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court issued a temporary order allowing construction of Trump's White House ballroom to continue for now.
  • Chief Justice John Roberts signed the brief order, which paused a lower-court ruling that would have halted construction.
  • The order was procedural — it did not rule on the legality of the project and did not give a timeline for a final decision.
  • Lower courts had found the project required congressional approval; the D.C. Circuit affirmed this before the case reached the Supreme Court.
  • U.S. District Judge Richard Leon ordered above-ground ballroom construction stopped but allowed below-ground/national-security construction to proceed.
  • The ballroom is being built on the site of the former East Wing of the White House, which was demolished.
  • The National Trust for Historic Preservation sued to stop the project, arguing the president needed congressional approval.
  • Trump praised the decision on social media, calling the 'Military/Ballroom Complex' vital for national security and 'THE GREATEST OF ITS KIND.'
  • The Trump administration argued the ballroom is an 'integrated military complex' vitally required by national security and the president's safety.
  • Solicitor General D. John Sauer represented the administration and argued it was too late to stop the project, which he said was roughly 65% complete.
  • Trump originally said private donors would fund the ballroom, but taxpayer money has since been implicated in the project's costs.
  • Polling cited indicates the project is unpopular with the public.
