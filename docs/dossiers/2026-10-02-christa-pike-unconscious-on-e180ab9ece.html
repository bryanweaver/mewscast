Consensus Facts

  • Tennessee attempted to execute death row inmate Christa Pike by lethal injection on Wednesday night but was unable to put her to death.
  • Pike, 50, was sentenced to death for the 1995 murder of Colleen Slemmer, a crime she committed when she was 18.
  • Pike would have been the first woman executed in Tennessee in more than 200 years.
  • Pike survived two lethal doses of the drug pentobarbital and was taken by ambulance to a Nashville-area hospital.
  • As of Friday, Pike's attorneys said she was critically ill, unconscious, intubated and on a ventilator.
  • Republican Gov. Bill Lee ordered an independent/third-party review of the failed execution and halted all remaining executions in the state for the year.
  • At least seven needles were used in attempts to establish IV access, and Pike at one point tried to help officials locate a vein.
  • Pike could be heard crying, whimpering, snoring and/or breathing loudly during the procedure, and she said her arm felt like it was about to burst or explode.
  • Pike's attorneys filed an emergency motion Friday asking the state to preserve all evidence related to the failed execution attempt.
  • Pike's legal team had warned before the execution that her preexisting conditions, including thrombocytosis and small veins, raised the risk of unnecessary pain and suffering.
  • Media witnesses reported that curtains to the execution chamber were closed at multiple points, limiting what they could observe.
  • The Tennessee Department of Correction stated it followed its lawful, established execution protocol, which does not allow more than two rounds of pentobarbital.
  • Multiple last-minute legal challenges, including two efforts the U.S. Supreme Court declined to grant, failed to stop the execution before it proceeded.
  • Pike's attorneys are seeking to have her death sentence commuted following the failed execution.
