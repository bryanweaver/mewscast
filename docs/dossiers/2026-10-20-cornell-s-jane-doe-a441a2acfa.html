Consensus Facts

  • A former Cornell student known as Jane Doe sent a letter to Cornell's Faculty Senate stating she felt unsupported and disbelieved by university President Michael Kotlikoff.
  • The letter describes a second-hand account in which Kotlikoff allegedly responded to her report by asking why she waited so long to report the incident, relayed to Doe by the then-president of her sorority (Tri Delta).
  • Cornell disputed Doe's characterization in a statement, saying her account is 'incorrect, based on a documented first-hand account,' and that Kotlikoff's question was directed at bystanders who did not use Cornell's anonymous tip line, not at Doe herself.
  • Jane Doe reported the alleged assault to campus police on Nov. 8, 2024, about three weeks after it allegedly occurred at the Chi Phi fraternity house.
  • The alleged assault took place in October 2024, when Doe says she was assaulted by multiple men at a Chi Phi fraternity party.
  • Doe filed a civil lawsuit last month naming the university, the Chi Phi fraternity, seven of its then-members, and others including her sorority Delta Delta Delta (Tri Delta).
  • All seven former fraternity members have denied wrongdoing, and none has been charged with a crime.
  • The letter was sent ahead of the Faculty Senate's consideration of a no-confidence vote in Kotlikoff's administration.
  • A former president of the Chi Phi fraternity was dropped from the lawsuit after his attorney said he was mistakenly identified and was not serving as chapter president at the time of the alleged assault.
  • The letter was obtained independently by CBS News, NBC News, and the BBC (via Reuters).
