Consensus Facts

  • Two planes collided while taxiing at Miami International Airport on Saturday night, November 14, 2026, around 9 p.m. ET.
  • The aircraft involved were a Copa Airlines Boeing 737 and a UPS Boeing 767.
  • The wingtip of the Copa Airlines jet struck the nose of the UPS cargo plane, according to the FAA.
  • The Copa flight was carrying 166 passengers and six crew members (172 people total).
  • The Copa plane had arrived from Panama City, Panama, and was taxiing to the gate; the UPS plane was preparing to depart for Bogota, Colombia.
  • Air traffic controllers had instructed the UPS crew to give way to the Copa flight, per the FAA.
  • Two people were treated at the scene for minor injuries and were not hospitalized.
  • Greg Chin, a spokesman for the Miami-Dade Aviation Department, stated flights were routed to other taxiways with no disruption to airport operations.
  • Air traffic control audio captured a pilot saying 'Somebody hit us' and a UPS pilot saying they 'went too far' and 'clipped' the other plane 'with the wing.'
  • The FAA is investigating the collision.
  • Both Copa and UPS said they would cooperate with investigating authorities.
  • The collision occurred a day after a rare FAA nationwide ground stop on Friday that caused thousands of flight cancellations and delays.
  • Federal officials linked Friday's ground stop to problems with aging air traffic control telecommunications equipment, including router issues.
  • The collision occurred at the intersection of two taxiways.
