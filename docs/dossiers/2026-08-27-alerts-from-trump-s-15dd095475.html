Consensus Facts

  • The NTSB released a preliminary report on Thursday, Aug. 27, 2026, about an Aug. 4 incident in which President Trump's helicopter (Marine One) came close to a commercial passenger jet near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport.
  • Marine One's crew made calls/warnings that the helicopter was preparing to depart, but controllers at Reagan National did not receive them; recordings posted by ATC.com show the calls were made.
  • The Marine One crew tried to relay the required three-minute notification through the helicopter facility at Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling, but that relay attempt was also unsuccessful.
  • The two aircraft came within about 0.82 nautical miles (roughly 0.8 mile / 1.3 km) laterally and approximately 700 feet vertically at their closest point.
  • The other aircraft was an Envoy Air flight; its crew received a traffic warning from their collision-avoidance system after takeoff but did not visually spot Marine One.
  • About one week before the incident (on July 30), HMX-1 / Marine One personnel met with Reagan National tower staff to discuss communication issues; both sides agreed to use backup methods, including relaying notifications through another aircraft, if direct radio failed.
  • FAA technicians later determined there was inadequate line-of-sight coverage between the radio receiver site and where Marine One was operating; the radios were subsequently moved from a nearby residential neighborhood to the top of Reagan National's control tower.
  • Subsequent radio checks/tests after the equipment was moved showed no deficiencies.
  • The Pentagon has a longstanding agreement (dating to 2013) with the FAA to provide controllers a three-minute warning before the president's helicopter takes off.
  • The incident and separation rules trace to safety procedures implemented after the Jan. 29, 2025 midair collision between an airliner and an Army Black Hawk near the airport that killed 67 people.
  • No one was injured and neither aircraft was damaged.

Disagreements

Whether the 700-foot vertical separation met FAA standards
Fox News: Reports that the 700 feet of vertical separation met the applicable FAA standard, noting Class B airspace requires either 1.5 nautical miles lateral OR 500 feet vertical separation.
The Washington Post: Frames the event as briefly getting 'too close' and as seeming to violate safety procedures, without noting the vertical-separation standard was met.
Marine One's destination that day
The Washington Post: States the president was headed to Andrews Air Force Base.
Fox News: States Trump departed for a planned trip to Los Angeles (via Air Force One), with the jet bound for Pensacola, Florida.
Details of the initial radio calls
Fox News: Specifies the first call to Reagan's helicopter-control frequency was not received and a second transmission was 'broken and completely unreadable.'
The Washington Post: Quotes ATC.com audio of the Marine One pilot saying 'three minutes to life' and the controller's surprised response ('no three minutes, roger, proceed as briefed').
