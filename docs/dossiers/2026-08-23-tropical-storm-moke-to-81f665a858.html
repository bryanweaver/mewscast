Consensus Facts

  • Tropical Storm Moke is approaching Hawaii and is forecast to pass to the south of the Hawaiian islands rather than make landfall (NPR, Al Jazeera, CNN, Newsweek).
  • Moke arrives less than a week after Hurricane/Tropical Storm Lala struck Hawaii, and its rainfall threat is amplified because it falls on already-saturated, recently-flooded ground (NPR, Al Jazeera, CNN, Newsweek).
  • The National Hurricane Center warned the rainfall may produce 'life-threatening flooding and mudslides' for the windward and southeast Big Island, especially in steep/deep terrain and areas recently affected by Lala (NPR, CNN, Newsweek).
  • Up to 15 inches of rain is possible on the Big Island, with eastern Maui expecting roughly 3 to 6 inches (NPR, CNN, Newsweek).
  • Lala did not make landfall but dropped extreme rainfall — reported at more than 30 inches / roughly 39 inches / over 3.5 feet in some areas — triggering floods and mudslides that damaged roads, bridges and homes (NPR, Al Jazeera, Newsweek).
  • At least one person died in connection with Lala (NPR, Al Jazeera, Newsweek).
  • Lala caused widespread power outages, with reports of roughly 100,000 to 130,000 utility customers losing power (Al Jazeera, Newsweek; NPR reports more than 8,000 still without power as of Saturday morning).
  • Local officials urged residents to prepare for Moke and warned of hazardous conditions, power and communication interruptions (NPR, Newsweek).
  • The NHC advisory referenced was issued at 11 p.m. local time Saturday (NPR, Newsweek).
