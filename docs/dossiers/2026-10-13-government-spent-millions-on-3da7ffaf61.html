Consensus Facts

  • Federal agents searched/raided the home of Rep. Ilhan Omar (D-Minn.) in Washington, D.C., on the morning of Monday, Oct. 12, 2026 (reported by CNBC and NBC News body text).
  • The search was conducted by Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), a division of the Department of Homeland Security, under a judicially authorized search warrant secured by the Department of Justice (CNBC and NBC News).
  • The Justice Department confirmed in a statement that HSI 'executed a judicially authorized search warrant' as part of an ongoing federal criminal investigation, without specifying details tying it publicly to Omar (CNBC and NBC News).
  • The investigation is a yearslong/complex criminal probe that involves allegations of fraud and matters partly related to immigration (CNBC and NBC News).
  • Omar has not been charged with a crime (CNBC; NBC News notes she is under investigation but reports no charges).
  • Omar responded on X, calling the action an attempt to 'target political enemies and weaponize federal law enforcement,' saying Trump has targeted her for a decade with 'baseless lies and smear campaigns,' and declaring 'I will not be silenced' (CNBC and NBC News).
  • House Democratic leaders Hakeem Jeffries, Katherine Clark and Pete Aguilar issued a joint statement calling the search 'deeply disturbing' and alleging 'corrupt weaponization of the federal government' (CNBC and NBC News).
  • The search was first reported by Fox News (CNBC and NBC News).
  • Omar immigrated to the U.S. from Somalia as a child and is running for reelection; the search came roughly three weeks before the Nov. 3 midterm elections (CNBC and NBC News).
  • A long-standing DOJ practice/policy counsels caution about investigative steps near an election that could appear to help or hurt a candidate (CNBC and NBC News).
