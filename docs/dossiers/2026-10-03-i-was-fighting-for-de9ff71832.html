Consensus Facts

  • An Indian airline captain, Smit Machchhar, was at the controls of a FlyDubai flight when his co-pilot stabbed him in an apparent attempt to crash the plane on Wednesday.
  • The flight was carrying 182 people from Dubai to Tel Aviv when the attack occurred.
  • Despite being seriously injured, Machchhar managed to open the cockpit door, allowing passengers to enter and restrain the attacker as the Boeing 737 descended sharply.
  • The flight was diverted and made an emergency landing in Tabuk, northwestern Saudi Arabia.
  • FlyDubai said the aircraft was secured by on-duty crew who diverted and landed the aircraft safely.
  • Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi hailed Machchhar as a hero, praising his courage, patience and presence of mind for saving lives.
  • Machchhar spoke to Modi in a televised/video call on Friday from a hospital in Abu Dhabi, where he is recovering; he said he was 'fighting for my life.'
  • Machchhar is from Mumbai, is married with two children, and trained as a pilot at Mainland Air in New Zealand.
  • Before FlyDubai (about four years), Machchhar spent 11 years with SpiceJet, logging more than 9,750 flying hours.
  • Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the attacker was an Omani national who was arrested in Saudi Arabia.
  • Machchhar discussed his career in a 2025 'Bona-Fide Banter' podcast interview, noting he initially enrolled in a textile engineering course.
  • Mainland Air head Philip Kean praised Machchhar and said pilot training does not typically prepare crew for a stabbing attack by a colleague.
