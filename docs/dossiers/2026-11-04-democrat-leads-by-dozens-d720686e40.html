Consensus Facts

  • Democrat Scott Colom, a district attorney, is in an extremely tight US Senate race against Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi.
  • As of Wednesday morning with roughly 96% of the expected vote counted, Colom led Hyde-Smith by about 65 votes (462,857 to 462,792), with both candidates at 48.9%.
  • Independent candidate Ty Pinkins held about 2% of the vote with 19,938 votes.
  • The race remains uncalled and too close to call, with tens of thousands of ballots still outstanding.
  • Mississippi has not elected a Democrat to the US Senate since 1982.
  • If Colom wins, he would be the first Black US senator from Mississippi since Reconstruction (1875).
  • Donald Trump won Mississippi in 2024 by roughly 20+ points and campaigned alongside Hyde-Smith the previous month.
  • Hyde-Smith took office by appointment in 2018 and was later elected to the Senate.
  • National Democrats directed additional funding to the Mississippi race in recent weeks, seeing an unexpected pickup opportunity.
  • Colom campaigned on cost-of-living and economic/health care concerns.
  • Mississippi allows mail-in ballots postmarked by Election Day to be counted if received within five business days afterward.
  • The surprisingly close result comes amid broader Democratic Senate gains, including flips in states Trump won by double digits, giving Democrats control of the chamber in the new year.
