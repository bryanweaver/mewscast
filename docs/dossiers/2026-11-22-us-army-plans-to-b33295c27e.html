Consensus Facts

  • The US Army plans to conduct a controlled detonation of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa.
  • The detonation involves roughly 68-70 drums containing nearly five tons (about 10,000 pounds) of lead azide, a compound used to initiate/detonate munitions.
  • The material was discovered to be degrading during a routine stockpile/safety review in March, after sitting in storage for about 20 years.
  • The EPA issued an emergency permit authorizing the detonation.
  • Environmental groups — the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club's Iowa chapter — sued/petitioned a federal appeals court to overturn the permit, arguing inadequate public notice, insufficient safeguards, and failure to consider safer disposal alternatives.
  • The Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals granted an administrative stay temporarily pausing the EPA permit on Thursday.
  • The Justice Department asked the appeals court to lift the pause and argued the Army can proceed under separate legal authority, citing an EPA permitting exemption for emergency situations requiring immediate action.
  • The detonation was originally scheduled for Friday, then rescheduled to Monday, then Tuesday, then to Wednesday.
  • The Army said the move to Wednesday was to allow for optimal weather conditions and all mitigation measures to be emplaced.
  • The Army argues the degraded lead azide is extremely unstable and sensitive to friction/shock, that dropping temperatures increase the risk of an uncontrolled explosion, and that safely destroying it in place is the only responsible option.
  • The planned detonation is expected to produce a dust plume containing some lead, loud noises, ground vibrations, and possibly broken windows.
  • Officials and health experts noted lead exposure is unhealthy at any level and especially dangerous to children.
  • Patrick O'Shaughnessy, a University of Iowa environmental health professor, called the situation unprecedented and will monitor the fallout.
