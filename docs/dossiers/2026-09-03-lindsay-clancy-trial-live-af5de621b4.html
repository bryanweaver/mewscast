Consensus Facts

  • The jury in Lindsay Clancy's murder trial deliberated without reaching a unanimous verdict, with deliberations extending into a sixth day (NBC News, ABC News, CBS News, CNN, USA Today).
  • The jury reported being deadlocked a second time before Judge William Sullivan gave a Tuey-Rodriguez instruction (also called a 'dynamite instruction' or Allen-type charge) urging them to keep trying to reach a verdict (NBC News, ABC News, USA Today).
  • The jury is composed of nine women and three men (NBC News, USA Today).
  • Clancy is charged in the January 2023 strangulation deaths of her three children — Cora, 5; Dawson, 3; and Callan, 8 months (NBC News, USA Today).
  • After the killings, Clancy attempted suicide by jumping from a second-story window, leaving her paralyzed (NBC News, USA Today).
  • The defense, led by attorney Kevin Reddington, argues Clancy lacks criminal responsibility due to postpartum psychosis and does not deny that she killed the children (NBC News, USA Today).
  • Prosecutors allege Clancy planned the killings, including sending her husband out on errands, and strangled the children with exercise bands (NBC News, USA Today).
  • If the jury cannot reach a verdict, Judge Sullivan will declare a mistrial, after which prosecutors must decide whether to retry Clancy (NBC News, ABC News, USA Today, CNN).
  • Reddington told reporters he expected prosecutors to retry the case if a mistrial is declared and said the defense would be ready (NBC News, USA Today).
