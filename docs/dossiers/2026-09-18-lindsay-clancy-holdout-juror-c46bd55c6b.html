Consensus Facts

  • A judge (William Sullivan) declared a mistrial in Lindsay Clancy's trial on September 4, 2026, after the jury failed to reach a unanimous verdict.
  • The jury was made up of 12 people — nine women and three men.
  • The jury was split 11-1, with the majority favoring finding Clancy not criminally responsible.
  • The lone holdout juror, Michael Péguy Desronvil, released a statement (via his attorney) saying he 'didn't have any doubts' that Clancy knew what she was doing and planned the killings.
  • Desronvil said in his statement: 'As I tried to explain different possible theories during deliberation, I kept getting cut off as if I had doubts based on the evidence presented.'
  • Clancy admitted to killing her three children — Cora, 5; Dawson, 3; and Callan, 8 months — by strangling them at the family's Duxbury, Massachusetts home in January 2023.
  • Clancy's defense, led by attorney Kevin Reddington, argued she was suffering from postpartum psychosis and should not be held criminally responsible; prosecutors argued the killings were calculated and planned.
  • Deliberations lasted roughly 38-40 hours before the mistrial was declared.
  • Other jurors, including the foreperson, said Desronvil acknowledged having reasonable doubt but refused to vote not guilty by reason of insanity / lack of criminal responsibility.
  • Reddington unsuccessfully sought to have the holdout juror removed during deliberations.
  • Prosecutors have not yet decided whether to seek a retrial; the next court hearing is scheduled for September 29 at Plymouth Superior Court.
  • Patrick Clancy, Lindsay's ex-husband, is set to speak publicly about the case for the first time on CBS's '60 Minutes' on Sunday.
  • Attorney Edward Paltzik said he represents Desronvil, described him as a 'devout Catholic' and 'devoted father,' and called him an 'American Hero and Champion of Justice' while asking the public to respect his privacy.
