Consensus Facts

  • In July 2026, DHS claimed Nevada had as many as 15,903 non-citizens on its voter rolls.
  • In an August meeting with Nevada election officials, DHS acknowledged it had manually reviewed only a fraction of those cases and confirmed just 185 registrants as non-citizens.
  • More than 14,000 flagged cases remained unresolved, and DHS separately identified roughly 6,200 (6,218) as 'higher confidence matches' not yet manually reviewed.
  • DHS official Kimberley Vogt told Nevada officials the 15,903 figure was 'preliminary' and had not undergone a 'full manual review' of each name.
  • DHS reached its figures by comparing Nevada's public voter rolls with federal immigration records.
  • Nevada Secretary of State Francisco (Cisco) Aguilar, a Democrat, said the federal government made sweeping public claims without providing the underlying proof.
  • DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin publicly announced in July that DHS identified roughly 250,000 non-citizens registered to vote across four states (Nevada, Pennsylvania, New Jersey and California).
  • This week the US Census Bureau released an analysis claiming roughly 24,000 non-citizens voted in the 2020 election, which Trump promoted; experts criticized its methodology and transparency.
  • David Becker of the Center for Election Innovation & Research criticized the administration for making baseless claims without showing its methodology.
  • During the August meeting, Nevada chief deputy secretary of state C. Murphy Hebert pressed DHS on whether the 15,000 number had been vetted, and Vogt characterized it as a ceiling that could only decrease.
