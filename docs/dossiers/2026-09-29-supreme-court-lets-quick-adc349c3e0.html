Consensus Facts

  • On Tuesday, September 29, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court granted the Trump administration's emergency request to resume deportations of immigrants to 'third countries' where they have no ties, staying a lower court ruling.
  • The Supreme Court's three liberal justices — Sonia Sotomayor, Elena Kagan and Ketanji Brown Jackson — indicated they would have denied the administration's request.
  • The order was unsigned, as is typical for emergency (shadow) docket petitions, and the court said it will hear oral arguments in the case in December 2026.
  • The lower court ruling being stayed came from U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy in Boston/Massachusetts, who held that migrants must be given a meaningful opportunity to raise safety concerns (torture/persecution) before removal, and much of it was upheld by a federal appeals court.
  • The Supreme Court previously (in 2025) allowed third-country deportations to proceed after an earlier Murphy order, including clearing the way for deportations to South Sudan.
  • The Trump administration argued the lower court decisions impeded efficient removal operations, including citing cancelled deportation flights.
  • James Percival, general counsel of the Department of Homeland Security, cheered the decision, calling it a win and urging people to self-deport with an offer of '$3,000 and a flight home.'
  • Roughly 25,000 people have been subject to third-country deportations, according to figures cited by the administration and rights groups.
  • Deportees have been sent to countries including South Sudan, and destinations cited include Eswatini, Rwanda, the Central African Republic, El Salvador and Liberia.
  • The Trump administration's March 2025 policy allows officials to send deportees to third countries based on blanket assurances those countries will not persecute or torture them.

CBS News: A February order from a federal district judge, largely upheld by a federal appeals court earlier this month.
Axios: A February 2026 order from Judge Murphy, much of which was backed by a recent appeals court decision; notes a prior stay followed an April 2025 Murphy order.

Number of flights/migrants affected by the cancellation
