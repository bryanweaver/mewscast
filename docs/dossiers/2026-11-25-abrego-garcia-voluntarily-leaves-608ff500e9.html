Consensus Facts

  • Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a 31-year-old Salvadoran national, voluntarily left the United States for Spain on Tuesday (departing from Washington Dulles International Airport).
  • Abrego Garcia was arrested in Baltimore in March 2025 and deported to a mega-prison in El Salvador known as CECOT, which activists describe as rife with abuses.
  • The Trump administration admitted in court that his deportation to El Salvador was an 'administrative error' / clerical error.
  • In 2019, an immigration judge ruled he could not be deported to El Salvador because he faced danger there from a gang that had threatened his family; he was allowed to live and work in the US under ICE supervision but given no residency status.
  • He immigrated to the US illegally as a teenager (around 2011) and has lived in Maryland for years; he has an American wife and a US-citizen child.
  • The administration brought him back to the US in June 2025, after public pressure and a court order, but only after securing an indictment charging him with human smuggling in Tennessee.
  • A federal judge dismissed the human smuggling case in May 2026, finding the prosecution was vindictive/tainted; the government appealed.
  • The administration sought to deport him to a third country, most recently Liberia, after proposing other African countries (Uganda, Eswatini, Ghana); he had argued he should be sent to Costa Rica.
  • Todd Lyons, former acting ICE director, wrote in a March 2026 memo that deporting him to Costa Rica would be 'prejudicial to the United States' and that he should be sent to Liberia.
  • A federal judge ordered Abrego Garcia to self-deport by Tuesday, according to a source familiar with the matter.
  • Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen, who met Abrego Garcia at CECOT while pressing for his release, said he is 'traveling to a country of his choice, a free man' and free from the administration's 'constant persecution.'
  • The Department of Homeland Security announced/confirmed the departure; Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin characterized it as a self-deportation and expressed being 'delighted.'
  • Trump and immigration officials alleged Abrego Garcia was an MS-13 gang member, an accusation he has repeatedly denied.
  • Representatives from the advocacy group We Are CASA accompanied Abrego Garcia and his family at the airport.
  • His attorney Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg characterized the departure as temporary, saying Abrego Garcia has options to return as the husband and father of US citizens and expects to come back under a future administration.

Disagreements

Nature of the departure — voluntary choice vs. negotiated agreement
Associated Press: DHS said he self-deported as part of a removal order, received no incentives, and won't be allowed back; attorney frames it as a temporary departure to wait out the administration.
NBC News: The Washington Post reported he voluntarily agreed to leave under an agreement with US authorities that would end removal proceedings; notes Maryland court records had yet to reflect such an order, with several sealed filings in recent weeks.
CNN: Frames it simply as a voluntary departure; notes he could not voluntarily leave to Costa Rica due to release conditions imposed by the judge.
Who the dismissing judge/prosecutor was tied to
CNN: Attributes the 'tainted investigation' finding to Attorney General Todd Blanche.
Associated Press: Says a federal judge found the DOJ's pursuit was designed to punish him for challenging his deportation; no AG named.
NBC News: Says the judge found the administration failed to rebut the 'presumption of vindictiveness'; no AG named.
Basis / details of the MS-13 gang allegation
NBC News: Details a 2019 Home Depot parking lot loitering encounter in Prince George's County where authorities cited his clothing (a Chicago Bulls hat), tattoos, and a confidential informant; lawyers called it 'hearsay.'
Al Jazeera English: States the administration argued he was MS-13 despite him having no criminal record at the time of removal.
Associated Press: Notes only that Mullin listed allegations including gang membership, which Abrego Garcia denies.
Framing of the Tennessee smuggling case origin
NBC News: Details it stemmed from a 2022 traffic stop for alleged speeding, with nine passengers he said he was taking to a construction job, and notes Tennessee authorities did not pursue charges at the time.
Associated Press: Describes it only as a human smuggling indictment used to secure his return.
CNN: Describes it as human smuggling charges filed on his return, later dismissed.
