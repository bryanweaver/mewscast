Consensus Facts

  • Pope Leo XIV, 71, was diagnosed with lung cancer after surgery at Rome's Gemelli hospital on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, according to the Vatican.
  • Surgeons removed the lower lobe of the pope's right lung.
  • The diagnosis was lung adenocarcinoma, confirmed by intraoperative histological examination and consistent with preoperative tests.
  • Adenocarcinoma is a type of non-small cell lung cancer that tends to grow more slowly than other kinds.
  • The surgery itself lasted approximately 90 minutes and the Vatican described it as successful with no complications.
  • The surgery was led by thoracic surgeon Professor Stefano Margaritora.
  • The pope returned to his room after surgery and is expected to recover for about a week.
  • The Vatican canceled all of Leo's audiences through October 20.
  • Leo first disclosed the discovery of a small lung nodule during his Sunday noon prayer in St. Peter's Square and announced he would have it removed.
  • Leo is the first American pope, a Chicago native, previously Cardinal Robert Prevost.
  • Leo is physically active, known for swimming and tennis, with no reported history of serious illness.
  • Leo was scheduled for a Latin America tour November 6-17 with stops in Uruguay, Argentina and Peru; it is unclear whether recovery will affect the itinerary.
  • The rosary was recited in the hospital chapel, led by Cardinal Baldassare Reina, Vicar General for the Diocese of Rome.
  • It is not clear whether the pope will undergo additional treatment such as chemotherapy, pending results of further tests.
