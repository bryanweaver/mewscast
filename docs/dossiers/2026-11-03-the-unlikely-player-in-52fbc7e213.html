Consensus Facts

  • Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens, who lost the Michigan U.S. Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, endorsed Republican nominee Mike Rogers on Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026.
  • The endorsement came two days before Election Day on Tuesday.
  • Stevens announced the endorsement in a video posted to social media, saying El-Sayed had told her she was no longer welcome in his campaign and that she should stay away, adding 'And he's right.'
  • Stevens made the endorsement after attending a church service in the Detroit area alongside Mike Rogers.
  • Stevens had been scheduled to appear at a joint unity event with El-Sayed (and others, including Rep. Jim Clyburn) on Sunday, but El-Sayed's campaign asked her to stay away.
  • The dispute escalated over an outside/liberal-group ad critical of Israel; Stevens wanted El-Sayed to condemn it, and El-Sayed said he had nothing to do with its contents.
  • El-Sayed dismissed the endorsement, telling reporters 'I don't think about Haley Stevens' and 'None of this matters.'
  • Mike Rogers said he was 'honored' by Stevens's support.
  • The seat is currently held by retiring Democratic Sen. Gary Peters, and the Michigan race is one of the most competitive contests that could determine control of the Senate.
  • Stevens is a centrist, pro-Israel Democrat, while El-Sayed represents the party's left wing and is a critic of U.S. support for Israel.
  • Members of Stevens's staff/team resigned following her announcement.
  • Michigan Democratic Party chair Curtis Hertel condemned the move, stating Stevens is not and will not be a member of the Michigan Democratic Party.
  • El-Sayed is a former Michigan public health official and the son of Egyptian immigrants; Rogers is an ex-congressman backed by President Donald Trump.
