Consensus Facts

  • The Department of Justice has begun issuing a new wave of subpoenas in a criminal probe tied to Alaska's U.S. Senate race (ABC News, CNBC, CBS News).
  • The subpoenas target Democratic officials believed to be tied to efforts to recruit Daniel J. Sullivan, a retired teacher, to appear on Alaska's ballot alongside incumbent Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan (ABC News, CBS News).
  • The probe follows a New York Times report published Wednesday detailing alleged efforts by people associated with Mary Peltola's campaign and the DSCC to recruit a second Dan Sullivan as a spoiler candidate (ABC News, CNBC, CNN, CBS News).
  • The DOJ amended its civil lawsuit seeking to block certification of Alaska's Senate results if Sen. Sullivan loses due to 'voter confusion,' incorporating the Times reporting and adding a new claim under the Constitution's Supremacy Clause (CNBC, CBS News).
  • Republican Alaska Gov. Mike Dunleavy ordered the state Department of Public Safety to investigate whether the alleged recruitment effort violated state criminal or civil law (ABC News, CNBC, CNN, CBS News).
  • Peltola's campaign (Alaskans for Mary) and the DSCC have denied any involvement with Daniel J. Sullivan's candidacy (ABC News, CNBC, CBS News).
  • Alaska's Supreme Court previously affirmed/greenlit Daniel J. Sullivan's candidacy as legal, declining in June to remove him from the ballot (ABC News, CNN, CBS News).
  • President Donald Trump has publicly called for Peltola to be prosecuted and/or removed from the ballot, and said he ordered the DOJ to file the lawsuit (ABC News, CNBC, CNN).
  • The Senate race could determine the balance of power in the Senate, where Republicans hold a narrow majority; Election Day is Nov. 3 (CNBC, CNN).
  • No one involved in the race has been charged with a crime (CBS News, and consistent with ABC News framing).
