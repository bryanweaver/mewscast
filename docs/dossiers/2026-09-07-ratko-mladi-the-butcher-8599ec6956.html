Consensus Facts

  • Ratko Mladić, the Bosnian Serb commander known as the 'Butcher of Bosnia', was buried in Belgrade, Serbia on Monday, September 7, 2026, with thousands of mourners attending.
  • Mladić died on August 27, 2026, at age 84 while serving a life sentence in The Hague, Netherlands, for genocide, war crimes and crimes against humanity.
  • Mladić was found guilty of genocide for his role in the 1995 Srebrenica massacre, in which around 8,000 Bosniak Muslim men and boys were killed.
  • The funeral service was led by the head of the Serbian Orthodox Church, Patriarch Porfirije, who said Mladić's name would remain 'deeply etched in the memory and prayers' of Serbian Orthodox people who feel gratitude toward him.
  • Mladić's coffin was flown to Serbia last week on a government plane, draped in national flags and carried by uniformed soldiers.
  • His military officer's cap and a sword/saber rested on the coffin, and he was buried with military honors.
  • The European Union denounced the 'glorification' of Mladić, stating it is incompatible with EU values and has no place in the EU or EU candidate countries.
  • EU enlargement commissioner Marta Kos canceled a planned visit to Serbia in protest, saying the return of his remains reminds Europe of 'the darkest pages in our recent history'.
  • Serbia is a candidate for EU membership (negotiating since 2012/2014 per varying accounts), but the accession process has stalled in recent years.
  • President Aleksandar Vučić, described as an ultranationalist/populist, did not attend the funeral, citing a prior engagement, but Serbian government ministers and Russia's ambassador to Serbia attended.
  • On Sunday evening, Red Star Belgrade football fans displayed a huge image of Mladić and chanted his name during a match against Partizan Belgrade.
  • Mladić remains viewed by many Serbs as a national hero and defender.
  • Mladić was arrested in 2011 after 16 years as a fugitive.
  • Mladić's crimes included the years-long siege of Sarajevo and other war crimes.
  • In Srebrenica, a UN-designated 'safe haven', men and boys were overrun by Serb forces, killed, and their bodies bulldozed into mass graves.

Disagreements

Was this a state funeral?
BBC News: A Serbian government minister initially said Mladić would be given a state funeral, but those plans were changed after objections from some European leaders.
The Guardian: Monday's ceremony was not an official state funeral, probably to avoid further damaging international relations, but it mirrored a state event with large police and military delegations.
CNN: Notes the funeral arrangements were formally made by Mladić's family, though the Serbian government sent a plane; a military commemoration was held Saturday at an official Serbian army venue.
Sarajevo siege death toll
BBC News: The siege of Sarajevo resulted in the deaths of more than 10,000 people.
The Irish Times: The 1,460-day siege of Sarajevo killed more than 10,000 people.
CNN: Does not give a specific Sarajevo figure; describes it as a years-long siege.
Number of people displaced by the Bosnian war
CNN: The conflict left more than 100,000 people dead and over 1 million homeless.
The Irish Times: The war killed more than 100,000 people and displaced more than 2 million.
Year Mladić was sentenced to life
CNN: Sentenced in 2017.
ABC News: Sentenced in 2017.
The Irish Times: Sentenced by a UN tribunal in 2017.
BBC News: Sentenced to life in prison in 2021 after a nine-year trial.
Year Serbia became/began EU candidacy
BBC News: Serbia has been a candidate to join the EU since 2012.
The Guardian: Serbia has been negotiating EU accession since 2014.
The Irish Times: Serbia has been negotiating EU accession since 2014.
Snap election framing and its connection to the funeral
The Guardian: Serbia's government announced intent to dissolve parliament and set a snap election; a Belgrade professor said he did not believe the government planned the announcement to coincide with the funeral; Vučić is nominated for prime minister as he cannot run for president again.
CNN: Vučić has stepped up anti-Western rhetoric ahead of an early parliamentary election expected next month.
The Irish Times: Does not tie the funeral to an election announcement.
ABC News, BBC News, Al Jazeera English: Do not mention the snap election.
