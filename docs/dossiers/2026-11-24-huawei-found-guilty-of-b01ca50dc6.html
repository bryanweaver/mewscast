Consensus Facts

  • A US jury in New York's Eastern District (Brooklyn) convicted Huawei of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and bank fraud.
  • The jury acquitted Huawei of the most serious charge, a criminal racketeering (RICO) conspiracy.
  • Huawei faced 12 charges and was convicted on 10 of them (BBC and Financial Times both state 10 of 12).
  • The trial lasted roughly two months.
  • Huawei's defense attorney Brian Heberlig said the company plans to appeal and characterized the racketeering acquittal as a rejection of the government's central narrative.
  • Prosecutors alleged Huawei encouraged its workforce of roughly 200,000 employees across about 170 countries to lie, cheat, and steal for competitive advantage.
  • The case traces back to the December 2018 detention in Canada of Huawei CFO Meng Wanzhou (daughter of the company's founder) on a US warrant, which she fought for about three years before returning to China under a deal that dropped the charges.
  • Prosecutors alleged Huawei stole technology from US companies including Cisco, T-Mobile, Quintel, and Fujitsu.
  • Huawei was accused of misleading banks including HSBC about its business dealings in Iran, in violation of US sanctions.
  • Huawei has been barred from selling equipment to US carriers for years over security concerns and was blacklisted by other Western allies including Canada and the UK.
  • Huawei issued a statement framing the racketeering acquittal as vindication and saying it would take further legal action.

Disagreements

NBC News: Guilty of seven conspiracy counts (bank fraud, wire fraud, money laundering, defrauding the US, conspiracy to steal trade secrets) plus bank fraud and wire fraud; acquitted of two counts — racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice.
Al Jazeera English: Convicted of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and bank fraud; acquitted of racketeering conspiracy; does not give a precise 10-of-12 count tally.
Sentencing date

Al Jazeera English: Does not specify a sentencing date.

NBC News: Quotes Huawei saying the charges are 'without legal or factual merit' and that it 'welcomes' the racketeering verdict.
