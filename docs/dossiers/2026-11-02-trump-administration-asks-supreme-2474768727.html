Suggested post type: REPORT — Four outlets with usable text reported the same Supreme Court appeal but with materially different emphasis — AP surfaced a parallel Elections Clause ruling others omitted, CNN foregrounded the Purcell principle and conservative majority, Al Jazeera uniquely named Justice Kagan, and CNBC gave the DOJ's legal theory the most space — making this a coverage-divergence story rather than a straightforward REPORT.

Consensus Facts

  • On Monday, November 2, 2026, the Trump administration/Department of Justice filed an emergency appeal to the US Supreme Court seeking to have California set aside mail-in ballots from roughly 20,000 voters the government suspects may not be US citizens.
  • The filing came one day before the November 3 midterm elections that will determine control of Congress.
  • On Friday, a 2-1 panel of the 9th US Circuit Court of Appeals rejected the administration's request; the majority opinion was from Circuit Judges Mark Bennett (a Trump nominee) and Gabriel Sanchez (a Biden nominee).
  • The 9th Circuit majority warned there is a 'material risk' that granting the requested relief would result in disenfranchisement.
  • A federal district judge, David Carter, had earlier denied the DOJ's request, warning the demand could disenfranchise citizens and writing that if the executive can discount votes due to unproven allegations, 'our democracy is in danger.'
  • Solicitor General D. John Sauer argued to the Supreme Court that once California commingles the challenged ballots with lawful ones, there is no way to retrieve them.
  • California officials say the government's list is unreliable and likely includes many US citizens, and that setting aside the ballots now would be logistically impossible.
  • California is a largely Democratic state where roughly 23 million registered voters are mailed ballots, and hundreds of thousands of ballots have already been processed.
  • There is no deadline for the Supreme Court to act; it could rule at any time.
  • Studies and audits show that ineligible voting by noncitizens is rare.
