Consensus Facts

  • German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's CDU suffered heavy defeats in two state elections held Sunday, Sept. 20, 2026, in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania (Mecklenburg-Vorpommern) and Berlin.
  • According to exit polls/projections by public broadcaster ARD, the CDU came in at just 5% in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania — described by multiple outlets as the party's lowest state-election result since World War II.
  • The far-right, anti-migration, Russia-friendly Alternative for Germany (AfD) made significant gains, leading in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania with roughly 37–38% of the vote.
  • Despite topping the vote in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, the AfD is not close to forming a regional government because other parties refuse to work with it.
  • In Berlin, the left-wing Die Linke (The Left) led the exit polls, with the CDU projected to come in second.
  • Merz publicly called the result a 'disaster' but vowed to stay on and press ahead with his government's planned economic, tax, pension and welfare reforms.
  • Merz said the reforms require 'backbone, steadfastness and patience' and framed them as an 'antidote' to the 'poison of authoritarianism.'
  • These elections came about two weeks after the AfD's historic state-election victory in Saxony-Anhalt on Sept. 6, 2026, which had already damaged Merz's standing.
  • Merz, 70, became chancellor 16 months prior with promises to revive Germany's stagnant economy but has become deeply unpopular.
  • AfD leaders (Alice Weidel and/or Tino Chrupalla) claimed the party had displaced or overtaken the CDU as Germany's 'people's party' and called on Merz to step down.
  • There have been calls, including from within the CDU, to potentially replace Merz, though no top-level allies publicly called for his departure.
