Consensus Facts

  • On Thursday, July 30, 2026, thousands of migrants streamed from Morocco into the small Spanish territory of Ceuta.
  • At least nine people died during the crossings (reported by CBS News, Al Jazeera, and Fox News).
  • Spain announced it would deploy its military/Armed Forces to support the Civil Guard in restoring order in Ceuta.
  • Local authorities in Ceuta, led by regional government head Juan Jesús Vivas, asked Madrid to declare a national emergency and send the army.
  • Spain's Interior Ministry said it would guarantee citizen safety and border integrity but that the government could not declare a national emergency over migration concerns.
  • Video footage showed crowds, mainly Moroccans and predominantly young men, walking around the breakwaters at Tarajal beach, with some families with women and children also present.
  • Some crossers shouted 'Viva España!' to a freelance photographer working for the Associated Press.
  • The scenes recalled the May 2021 Ceuta crisis, when more than 8,000 migrants entered in two days.
  • It was not immediately clear what prompted the surge; Ceuta officials pointed to a recent Spanish Supreme Court ruling limiting immediate pushbacks of sea arrivals, but some migration advocates questioned that explanation.
  • Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez and Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska were expected to visit Ceuta (Friday per CBS/Fox).
  • Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni threatened to suspend Italy's participation in the Schengen open-border agreement with Spain to defend Italy's borders.
