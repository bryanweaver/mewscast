Consensus Facts

  • Two candidates named Dan Sullivan appear on Alaska's U.S. Senate ballot: the Republican incumbent Sen. Dan Sullivan and Daniel J. Sullivan, a retired teacher.
  • The Department of Justice filed a civil lawsuit seeking to block or delay certification of the Alaska Senate race if Sen. Sullivan loses by a margin attributable to voter confusion over the two Sullivans.
  • The DOJ amended its civil complaint on Thursday to add details from a New York Times report and a new legal claim.
  • An ongoing criminal investigation into the Alaska Senate race has been underway for months and is being pursued by the DOJ.
  • The DOJ has issued a new wave of subpoenas targeting Democratic officials believed to be tied to recruiting Daniel J. Sullivan onto the ballot.
  • A New York Times report (published Wednesday) alleged that aides to Mary Peltola's campaign and senior aides at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee helped recruit the retired teacher to run.
  • Peltola's campaign and the DSCC have denied any involvement with Daniel J. Sullivan's candidacy.
  • U.S. Attorney Michael Heyman had been recused from the matter because he was being considered for a judicial nomination, and that recusal was rescinded after he was no longer under consideration.
  • Republican Alaska Gov. Mike Dunleavy ordered a state investigation into the alleged recruitment effort.
  • Alaska state election officials initially challenged Daniel J. Sullivan's eligibility, but state courts/Alaska's Supreme Court affirmed his candidacy was legal.
  • Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, and the winner of the Alaska race could determine Senate control.
  • No one involved in the race has been charged with a crime.
