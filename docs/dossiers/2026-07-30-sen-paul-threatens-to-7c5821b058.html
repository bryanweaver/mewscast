Consensus Facts

  • Dr. Anthony Fauci appeared before the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee on Wednesday, July 29, 2026, in Washington.
  • Fauci invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination and declined to answer questions about the COVID-19 pandemic.
  • The committee is chaired by Sen. Rand Paul (R-Ky.), who subpoenaed Fauci to testify.
  • Sen. Rand Paul said the committee will hold a vote next week on whether to hold Fauci in contempt of Congress.
  • Paul said there is a 'very real chance' Fauci could be prosecuted for not answering questions.
  • Before leaving office, President Joe Biden issued Fauci a preemptive pardon covering his actions from 2014 through early 2025.
  • Paul argues Fauci's Fifth Amendment invocation is 'unsupported' because the blanket pardon means he cannot be prosecuted for the matters at issue.
  • Days before the hearing, Paul released roughly 1,000+ pages (reported as 1,141 pages) of Fauci's pandemic-era diary entries.
  • Paul and Fauci have clashed for years over the origins of COVID-19, with Paul backing the lab-leak theory tied to the Wuhan Institute of Virology and Fauci favoring natural spillover.
  • Sen. Ron Johnson (R-Wis.) questioned Fauci and said he was unsurprised Fauci took the Fifth.
  • Democrats, including Sen. Maggie Hassan and Sen. Richard Blumenthal, defended Fauci and characterized the hearing as an attack on science or a 'show trial.'
  • Invoking the Fifth Amendment is not itself a crime and is routinely used in the US judicial system.
  • Fauci's defense lawyer, David Schertler, said Fauci is on 'solid legal ground' and that 'Rand Paul may be a doctor, but he is not a lawyer.'
  • President Trump publicly criticized Fauci, calling him 'crazy' and asserting the virus came from the Wuhan laboratory.
