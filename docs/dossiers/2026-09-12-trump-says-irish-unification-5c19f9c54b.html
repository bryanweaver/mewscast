Consensus Facts

  • During a visit to Dublin on Saturday, September 12, 2026, President Donald Trump said he would 'love' to see a united Ireland and called it a 'fantastic thing.'
  • Trump made the remarks while sitting next to Ireland's Taoiseach (Prime Minister) Micheál Martin.
  • Trump prefaced his comments by saying he didn't want to cause any problems/trouble, but predicted unification is 'going to happen eventually.'
  • Trump acknowledged that the UK 'will have something to say about it.'
  • Trump's remarks break with the precedent of prior US presidents, who have avoided explicitly endorsing Irish reunification (including Joe Biden, who often referenced his Irish heritage).
  • The 1998 Good Friday Agreement, which the US helped broker and which largely ended the Troubles, requires a majority vote in border polls in Northern Ireland for reunification to take place.
  • UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham had said the prior month that a referendum on Northern Ireland's status was 'off the table,' and that he was not aware of majority public support for another referendum.
  • After Trump's comments, Burnham's office/Downing Street said his position on Irish unity remained unchanged.
  • Gavin Robinson, leader of the Democratic Unionist Party (DUP), responded that Northern Ireland's future 'will be decided by the people of Northern Ireland, not by commentary in London, Dublin or Washington.'
  • Trump also met with Irish President Catherine Connolly during the visit and addressed US and Irish business leaders at the US ambassador's residence in Dublin.
  • Trump's visit combined diplomacy with attending a golf tournament (the Irish Open) at his Doonbeg golf course in western Ireland.
  • Anti-Trump protests took place in Dublin, Doonbeg, and at Shannon Airport during the visit.
