Consensus Facts

  • Todd Blanche is the acting attorney general and Trump's nominee to be the permanent attorney general.
  • Sen. John Cornyn, R-Texas, a member of the Senate Judiciary Committee, canceled a scheduled meeting with Blanche over concerns about the settlement of Trump's lawsuit against the IRS.
  • Cornyn told reporters he is 'not prepared to vote yes,' putting Blanche's committee advancement in doubt.
  • The Senate Judiciary Committee is scheduled to vote Thursday on whether to advance Blanche's nomination to the full Senate.
  • Sen. Thom Tillis, R-North Carolina, another Judiciary Committee Republican, shares Cornyn's concerns; a single Republican holdout could sink Blanche's advancement.
  • The core dispute involves a settlement provision that shields Trump, his family, and their business entities from IRS audits and enforcement actions for tax returns filed before the settlement, plus a roughly $1.8 billion 'anti-weaponization' fund to compensate people who claim they were wronged by the DOJ.
  • Blanche testified at his July 15 confirmation hearing that the 'weaponization fund is dead,' that the settlement does not extend immunity to non-parties, and that it does not apply to agencies beyond the IRS and Treasury.
  • Cornyn and Tillis are demanding a written commitment from the DOJ formally killing the fund and codifying Blanche's sworn testimony; Cornyn says he has not received responsive written assurances.
  • Committee Chairman Sen. Chuck Grassley, R-Iowa, set a roughly 4 p.m. ET Wednesday deadline to decide whether Thursday's vote could proceed.
  • The settlement resolved a lawsuit Trump filed against the IRS over the illegal leaking of his tax records by an agency contractor, who pleaded guilty in 2023.
