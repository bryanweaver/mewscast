Consensus Facts

  • Hurricane Lowell skirted along Hawaii's western edge early the week of Sept. 7-8, 2026, damaging Kauai's oceanfront resorts, roads, and power infrastructure.
  • Tourism officials urged visitors to stay away from Kauai for now while the island recovers.
  • Caroline Anderson, interim president and CEO of the Hawaii Tourism Authority, said in a written statement that officials hope visitors will adjust travel plans rather than cancel Hawaii trips altogether.
  • Kauai sees only a small fraction of Hawaii's nearly 10 million annual visitors, but tourism is its top economic driver, accounting for an estimated 4 out of every 10 jobs on the island (per local estimates).
  • Thousands of customers, including some resorts, remained without power, and crews continued clearing streets of downed trees and utility poles.
  • Extensive lines backed up at Kauai's primary airport as people tried to leave the island.
  • The storm was blamed for at least two deaths: a 74-year-old man found Wednesday near Kauai's south shore, and a man found dead Monday on Oahu after a large tree fell on his tent.
  • Democratic Gov. Josh Green asked President Donald Trump on Wednesday to issue a major disaster declaration to expedite federal help for Kauai and Maui counties.
  • On Lanai, storm damage rendered inoperable a harbor that supplies much of the food and fuel to the island's 3,300 residents.
  • Larry Ellison bought nearly all of Lanai in 2012 for about $300 million.
  • The Ko'a Kea Resort in Poipu said it would offer refunds for guests who had to cancel rather than reschedule, and expected to resume normal operations within two weeks.
  • The Sheraton Kauai Coconut Beach Resort, which sustained no structural damage, closed beginning Thursday evening but hoped to reopen soon after power is restored.
  • The storm surge buckled and washed away roads, knocked down home walls, and flooded resorts, sending rapids through a hotel lobby and scattering debris.
