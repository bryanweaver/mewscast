Consensus Facts

  • The USS Abraham Lincoln returned to its home port in the San Diego area (Naval Air Station North Island, Coronado) on Thursday, Oct. 8, 2026, after roughly a 10-month deployment.
  • The deployment set a U.S. Navy record for consecutive days at sea without a port call (reported as 264-265 days).
  • The carrier left San Diego in November 2025, was redirected to the Middle East, and supported combat operations against Iran (referred to as Operation Epic Fury).
  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth visited the carrier on Wednesday, Oct. 7, 2026, and announced the strike group would receive the Presidential Unit Citation.
  • Hegseth pushed back against media characterizations of the deployment as a symbol of dysfunction, low morale, or a failed mission.
  • Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao acknowledged that eight sailors from the Lincoln and its strike group attempted suicide during the deployment.
  • Reports surfaced of supply shortages (food, water, hygiene products), plumbing problems, low morale, and deteriorating mental health among the crew.
  • The citation credited the strike group with 3,693 combat missions/flights, 450 Tomahawk cruise missiles launched, 77 one-way attack drones shot down, and 64 Iranian warships sunk.
  • President Donald Trump, asked in August whether the deployment had gone on too long, said it was 'not nearly long enough.'
  • The carrier made a port call in Thailand on its way home before returning to San Diego.
  • The USS George Washington relieved the Lincoln in late August, allowing it to begin its journey home.
  • Families gathered at the pier, some before dawn, to welcome returning sailors.
