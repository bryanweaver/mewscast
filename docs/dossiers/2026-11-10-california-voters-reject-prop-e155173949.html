Suggested post type: REPORT — Three outlets (BBC, CBS News, The Guardian) reported the same result with materially different framings — ranging from an international 'world's first tax' angle to an inequality/billionaire-influence lens to an economic-flight narrative — and key figures like total spending and Brin's contribution diverge across sources with no primary document to reconcile them, making this a coverage-report story rather than a straight REPORT.

Consensus Facts

  • California voters rejected Proposition 40, a measure to tax the wealth of billionaires (confirmed by BBC, CBS News, and The Guardian body text).
  • Prop. 40 would have levied a one-time 5% tax on California residents with a net worth of more than $1 billion (confirmed by BBC, CBS News, and The Guardian).
  • Supporters framed the tax as a way to offset federal healthcare cuts stemming from President Trump's tax-and-spending law (the 'One Big Beautiful Bill Act' per CBS News and The Guardian; BBC references Trump's tax and spending bill).
  • Proponents projected the tax would raise roughly $100 billion (BBC says ~$100bn; CBS News says ~$100 billion over five years).
  • The measure was principally sponsored by the healthcare union SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West (confirmed by CBS News and The Guardian).
  • Governor Gavin Newsom opposed the measure, citing concerns that it would drive the wealthy out of the state and reduce income tax revenue (confirmed by BBC, CBS News, and The Guardian).
  • Google co-founder Sergey Brin was a leading donor to the opposition campaign, contributing more than $100 million (BBC cites more than $100m; The Guardian cites at least $132m; CBS News names Brin among opponents).
  • The opposition campaign was the most expensive of its kind in California history, with figures cited in the hundreds of millions of dollars (The Guardian cites ~$425m; CBS News cites over $250 million for Building a Better California as of Oct. 28).
  • Two companion measures, Propositions 41 and 42, were designed so that if either received more 'Yes' votes than Prop. 40, they would block the billionaire tax from taking effect even if it passed (confirmed by CBS News and The Guardian).
  • Proposition 41 was also rejected by voters (confirmed by CBS News).
  • The measure divided Democrats and traditional allies, including labor unions (confirmed by BBC, CBS News, and The Guardian).

Disagreements

Total opposition campaign spending
The Guardian: Billionaires mounted a nearly $425m battle against Prop 40, the most expensive opposition campaign in state history.
CBS News: Building a Better California raised over $250 million against the billionaire tax as of Oct. 28.
Associated Press (headline-only, via CalMatters headline): Billionaires spent $378 million to kill California's wealth tax.
Sergey Brin's contribution amount
BBC News: Brin gave more than $100m to a political organisation funding ballot measures that could block the tax.
The Guardian: Brin contributed at least $132m to fight the tax, per campaign finance filings.
Projected revenue from the tax
CBS News: Proponents projected $100 billion over five years, but a Stanford study concluded it may generate only about $30 billion.
BBC News: Would have raised about $100bn.
The Guardian: Does not provide a revenue projection figure.
Scope of what the tax applied to
BBC News: A 5% tax on total wealth, including assets such as shares as well as income.
CBS News: A 5% tax on net worth of $1 billion, excluding real estate and certain retirement assets.
The Guardian: A 5% tax on all individuals with a net worth of more than $1bn as of 1 January 2026.
Who opposed among Democratic figures
CBS News: Opposed by Newsom, former Rep. Katie Porter, the California Medical Association, and the California Teachers Association.
The Guardian: Opposed by both the Democratic and Republican candidates for governor and Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California; backed by Bernie Sanders and Ro Khanna.
