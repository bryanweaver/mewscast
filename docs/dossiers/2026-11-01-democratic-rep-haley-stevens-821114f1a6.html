Consensus Facts

  • Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens of Michigan endorsed Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers on Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026 (CBS News, NBC News).
  • Stevens appeared alongside Rogers at a church service in Detroit on Sunday (CBS News, NBC News).
  • In a video posted to social media, Stevens said Abdul El-Sayed told her she was no longer welcome in his campaign and should stay away, and said 'he's right,' which is why she was endorsing Rogers (CBS News, NBC News).
  • Stevens narrowly lost the August 2026 Michigan Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed (CBS News, NBC News, NYT).
  • The rift stems from Stevens calling on El-Sayed to condemn an ad from an outside progressive group (identified as Rebellion PAC) that she characterized as antisemitic for linking U.S. aid to Israel to high grocery prices (CBS News, NBC News, NYT).
  • El-Sayed responded by accusing Stevens of being 'intent on relitigating a primary that ended three months ago,' and his campaign distanced itself from the ad, saying he did not make it and is not responsible for it (CBS News, NBC News, NYT).
  • El-Sayed's campaign asked Stevens not to attend remaining campaign events in the final days before the election (CBS News, NBC News).
  • Stevens had been expected to appear with El-Sayed at a Sunday unity event alongside Rep. Jim Clyburn of South Carolina (CBS News, NBC News, NYT).
  • The Michigan Senate race between El-Sayed and Rogers is a competitive battleground contest that could help determine Senate control (CBS News, NBC News, NYT).
  • During the primary, Stevens was heavily backed by pro-Israel spending, including AIPAC-affiliated super PAC money (NBC News, NYT).
