Consensus Facts

  • Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis announced a naval blockade against Saudi Arabia this week (announced Monday, July 20/21), threatening tankers that use Saudi Arabian Red Sea ports.
  • Two Chinese supertankers carrying Saudi crude oil successfully exited the Bab el-Mandeb strait despite the Houthi blockade, per London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) vessel-tracking data.
  • Multiple oil tankers carrying Saudi crude made U-turns away from Houthi-controlled waters earlier in the week when the blockade began.
  • The Bab el-Mandeb strait is a major global shipping chokepoint used by a large share of maritime and energy traffic.
  • US President Donald Trump publicly commented on the situation, indicating the US would respond to Houthi threats against shipping.
  • The Saudi oil transiting the strait is destined primarily for Asian markets, including China and India.

Newsweek: Cites the Institute for the Study of War saying seven vessels changed course due to Houthi warnings, including the Xin Long Yang and a COSCO vehicle carrier.
Why the Chinese ships were allowed through
Newsweek: Attributes it to ship-by-ship transit clearances granted by the Houthis to COSCO, possibly tied to a broader Beijing-Houthi-Iran commercial understanding (per analysts Cichen Shen and Arif Gasilov); notes the ships had loaded oil before the blockade began.
The Guardian: Frames it as an open question, citing the Atlantic Council's Allison Minor that the Houthis 'may be less willing to cut deals with Chinese ships carrying Saudi oil,' contrasting with prior deals with Russia and China.

Emphasizes the diplomatic dimension — collapsed US-Iran interim deal, mediation efforts, possible 10-day ceasefire per Axios, Rubio's comments. Foregrounds expert commentary (Atlantic Council's Allison Minor) and situates the blockade within the longer 2023-2025 Houthi Red Sea campaign and the Gaza war origins.
