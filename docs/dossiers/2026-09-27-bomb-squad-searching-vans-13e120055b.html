Consensus Facts

  • British police arrested five men on Sunday near RAF Fairford, an air base in Gloucestershire, western England, used by U.S. forces.
  • The men were arrested on suspicion of committing offenses under the Explosives Act and later further arrested on suspicion of preparing/preparation of a terrorist act.
  • Police received a call at approximately 12:45 a.m. GMT/local time about three suspicious vehicles that appeared to be traveling toward the base.
  • Around 85 households/homes near the base (in or near the village of Whelford) were evacuated as a precaution.
  • A 400-meter/440-yard cordon was put in place while the Army's Explosive Ordnance Disposal unit examined the vehicles.
  • Counter Terrorism Policing, led by Vicki Evans, took the lead on the investigation, which is in its 'early stages,' with the five men remaining in custody.
  • Gloucestershire Police Assistant Chief Constable Richard Ocone made public statements about the incident and the emergency response.
  • Aerial/helicopter footage showed a bomb disposal robot operating near three white vans close to the base.
  • President Donald Trump told reporters (during a trip to Chicago) that the arrests were 'fantastic' and 'an amazing job,' that the suspects were 'looking to do big damage,' and that the men had been under investigation/view for a long time in cooperation with British authorities.
  • Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he was receiving updates and that the incident would be 'deeply concerning' for local residents; Defence Secretary Wes Streeting thanked emergency and specialist responders.
  • RAF Fairford has been used by U.S. Air Force bombers (B-1/B-52) to launch strikes against Iran during the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran, and the U.K. government authorized such use.
  • Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps warned in July that any base used to launch attacks on Iran would be considered a legitimate target.
  • Authorities have not officially linked Sunday's arrests to Iran or confirmed a motive, and the men were not named in line with British policing rules.
  • The U.S. Air Force / U.S. European Command declined to discuss specific force protection measures but said it remains vigilant and would take steps to protect personnel and their families.
