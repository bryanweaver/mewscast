Consensus Facts

  • The Trump administration proposed a new wave of tariffs on China, the EU, and other major trading partners on November 25, 2026 (AP, Washington Post headline, CNN, CBS).
  • The tariffs target 14 trading partners in total, including China and the EU (Washington Post/CNBC aggregation, CNN, CBS; AP cites 'China, the European Union, Taiwan and 11 other countries').
  • The tariffs are a response to alleged foreign overcapacity/subsidized manufacturing that the administration says undercuts American firms (AP, CNN, CBS).
  • The proposal stems from months-long Office of the US Trade Representative investigations into foreign trade practices (CNN, CBS).
  • US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer is the named administration spokesperson, citing 'market-distorting practices that tilt the trade balance in their favor while eroding America's manufacturing base' (AP, CBS).
  • The new tariffs are designed to replace, not stack on top of, prior tariffs struck down earlier in the year (AP, CNN, CBS).
  • The Supreme Court earlier in 2026 (February) blocked Trump's sweeping emergency-powers tariffs, which this proposal is partly intended to work around and replace lost revenue (AP, CNN, CBS).
  • The tariffs require a public notice-and-comment process and hearings before taking effect, with implementation expected no earlier than January–March 2026 (AP, CNN, CBS).
  • The proposal includes exclusions/exemptions for certain categories such as semiconductors and other goods (AP cites semiconductors and high-tech goods from Taiwan/South Korea; CNN cites energy, food, certain chips, pharmaceuticals; CBS references targeted products).
  • Economists characterized the tariffs as having a modest impact, essentially returning rates to earlier-in-year levels (AP cites Capital Economics' Mark Williams; CBS cites Capital Economics' Stephen Brown).
  • Roughly two-thirds (66%) of voters said Trump's tariffs/policies hurt the economy, per post-midterm polling (AP Voter Poll, CNN Voter Poll, CBS News poll).
  • The proposal comes after Democrats won majorities in the House and Senate in the recent midterm elections, in which tariff frustration was a factor (AP, CNN, CBS).
  • Some Republican lawmakers, alongside Democrats, have publicly raised concerns about Trump's tariff policy (CNN names Sens. Ted Cruz and Todd Young; AP names Democratic Sen. Ron Wyden).
