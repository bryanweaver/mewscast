Consensus Facts

  • The COP31 UN climate conference in Antalya, Turkey ended on Sunday, November 22, 2026, without a final agreement.
  • The summit ran into overtime and became the longest COP on record.
  • The talks were co-hosted/co-presided by Turkey (conference president Murat Kurum) and Australia (Chris Bowen).
  • Murat Kurum confirmed at the closing plenary there was no consensus and that no new/further text would be advanced.
  • The European Union rejected the draft deal because it did not mention fossil fuels, with Ireland's minister Darragh O'Brien speaking for the EU.
  • Oil- and gas-producing countries, notably Saudi Arabia, opposed including references to 'fossil fuels' in the final text.
  • UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was 'deeply disappointed' and expressed 'very profound frustration' at the outcome.
  • This was the first time UN climate talks failed to reach an adopted deal since Copenhagen in 2009.
  • Disputes centered on two core issues: fossil fuel language and climate finance.
  • The draft deal would have acknowledged that global warming is on course to exceed the 1.5C (2.7F) threshold set in the 2015 Paris Agreement.
  • The draft included a goal to expand electricity's share of total final world energy consumption to roughly 35% by 2035.
  • The draft contained carbon-removal/carbon-capture language that critics worried would be used as a license to keep producing oil.
  • Nearly 200 countries participated in the negotiations.
