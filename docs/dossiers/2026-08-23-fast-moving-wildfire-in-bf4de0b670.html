Consensus Facts

  • A fast-moving wildfire named the Hawk Fire ignited Saturday (Aug. 22, 2026) on a hillside near Peavine Mountain in the Humboldt–Toiyabe National Forest just outside Reno, Nevada.
  • The fire was human-caused according to authorities, with the cause still under investigation (reported by ABC News, CBS News, The Guardian, and The Independent).
  • Approximately 90,000 residents were urged to evacuate, with 42,000 under mandatory 'GO NOW' orders and an additional 45,000 in an evacuation warning zone (ABC News, CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent).
  • Six people were injured, including three civilians and three first responders (ABC News, CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent).
  • The 'GO NOW' evacuation zone reached the edge of the University of Nevada, Reno campus (ABC News, CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent).
  • Nevada Gov. Joe Lombardo declared a state of emergency in Washoe County and mobilized the Nevada National Guard, including two helicopter crews and 60 troops (CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent, The Economic Times).
  • Strong, gusty winds, low humidity, and dry vegetation fueled the rapid, shifting spread of the fire (CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent, The Economic Times).
  • Multiple structures/homes were destroyed by the fire (ABC News, CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent).
  • Nearly 10,000 homes and businesses in Washoe County were without power on Sunday, and portions of U.S. Route 395 were closed (CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent).
  • The Reno-Sparks Convention Center was opened to evacuees (pets not permitted), and Reno casinos offered hotel room discounts to evacuees (CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent).
  • Reno has a population of more than 280,000 residents (CBS News, The Guardian, The Independent).
  • The Hawk Fire follows three wildfires earlier in the month that forced more than 13,000 residents north of Reno to evacuate (CBS News, The Guardian).
  • Richard Edwards, chief of Truckee Meadows Fire & Rescue, described the incident as 'very dynamic' and driven by winds moving rapidly into residential areas (CBS News, The Economic Times).
