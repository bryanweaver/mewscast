Consensus Facts

  • Vice President JD Vance told fellow Republicans to 'stop whining' about Vivek Ramaswamy's attack ad during campaign appearances in Ohio on Wednesday, Oct. 21, 2026.
  • When asked whether the ad was appropriate, Vance said it was 'not really my concern,' and said he was 'a little bit disgusted' by Republicans who criticized the ad more than they criticize Democrats.
  • At a rally, Vance praised Ramaswamy for having the 'audacity' to run a TV commercial that 'calls it out like it is' and 'calls Amy Acton a liar.'
  • The ad features Amy Acton's estranged mother, Donna Arthur, calling her a liar; Acton says she has not spoken to her mother in roughly 50 years (since she was 12, dating to a 1978 court case).
  • Acton has said she was sexually abused as a child by her mother's husband (her stepfather); charges were filed and Acton testified against him.
  • Ramaswamy confirmed on Tuesday that the ad came from his campaign and pledged roughly $4 million to air it statewide.
  • The ad drew bipartisan backlash, including from Republicans such as Sen. Jon Husted, who said he 'would not have run that ad.'
  • Acton's brother, Philip Stearns, condemned the ad, saying Ramaswamy 'has stooped to such a despicable low' by turning the sexual abuse into a political talking point, and appeared in a response ad from Acton's campaign saying 'Shame on Vivek.'
  • President Donald Trump, asked about the ad Wednesday, said he knew 'nothing about it' and that it was 'a very unusual situation to have a mother hate a daughter that much,' adding he didn't think it had anything to do with Ramaswamy.
  • The events occurred roughly two weeks (about 13 days) before the 2026 midterm elections, in a competitive Ohio governor's race.
