Consensus Facts

  • The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points on Wednesday, September 16, 2026, setting the target range at 3.75% to 4.00%.
  • This was the Fed's first rate increase since 2023 (described as the first in three years).
  • The vote to hike was unanimous among FOMC members.
  • The Fed signaled at least one additional rate hike could come before the end of the year.
  • Kevin Warsh is the Fed chairman and made the decision despite President Donald Trump's repeated demands for lower interest rates.
  • The Fed's post-meeting statement said the action 'will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal.'
  • The rate hike is a response to elevated inflation driven in part by soaring oil prices linked to the war with Iran/Middle East.
  • At his press conference, Warsh declined to discuss his conversations with the president, stating the Fed 'stays in its lane.'
  • Rising AI investment/competition for capital was cited as a factor in the inflation and rate environment.
  • Long-term Treasury yields were near multi-decade highs around the time of the decision.
