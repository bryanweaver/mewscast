Consensus Facts

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a new round of U.S. economic sanctions on Iran on Monday, August 24, 2026.
  • The administration framed the campaign as an 'economic D-Day,' with Bessent's stated objective to 'sever every economic lifeline that sustains the tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone.'
  • Bessent laid out the campaign in an op-ed for the Financial Times ahead of a press conference.
  • The sanctions expand 'secondary' sanctions targeting entities and countries that do business with Iran, spanning sectors including shipping, oil, digital assets/crypto, gold, and aviation.
  • Bessent said any entity facilitating money laundering for Iran would be removed from the U.S. dollar system, while also referencing a 'cure period' and declining to set a firm timeline ('we do not have infinite patience').
  • Bessent said President Trump was personally calling world leaders to request they cease trading with Iran.
  • The Iranian rial plunged to a record low around the time of the announcement.
  • The U.S. and Israel have been at war with Iran for nearly six months, beginning around February 2026, and a U.S. naval blockade plus the closure of the Strait of Hormuz has severely disrupted Iranian and regional oil exports.
  • The U.S. has imposed sanctions on Iran for decades, and Iran has repeatedly found ways to evade them via front companies.
  • Iranian officials publicly downplayed and/or threatened retaliation against the new sanctions, including warnings over the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Analysts and officials question the effectiveness of new sanctions given how heavily Iran is already sanctioned and the reliance on partners like China, Russia and India to cooperate.
