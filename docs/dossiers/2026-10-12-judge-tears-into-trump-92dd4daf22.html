Consensus Facts

  • U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes, a Biden appointee, held an emergency hearing Monday, Oct. 12, 2026, and said she was 'furious' with the Trump administration over tree removal at the East Potomac Golf Course in Washington, D.C.
  • The Trump administration removed roughly 430 trees from the course over the holiday weekend, with crews beginning work Saturday morning.
  • The Justice Department filed notice of the removal in court shortly before 5 p.m. Friday, at the start of the three-day holiday weekend, giving less than a day's notice to the court and the plaintiffs.
  • Reyes told the government it had 'lost' or 'cost' its 'credibility' and that she had 'trusted you all not to do precisely what you have done.'
  • When a DOJ attorney said the government had done its 'best' to notify the court, Reyes responded that they had done their 'absolute worst.'
  • Reyes issued a temporary order halting further tree removal (through Tuesday / roughly 24 hours), and the hearing lasted about three to four hours.
  • The tree removal is connected to a disputed plan to renovate the public course into a championship-level course, a plan being challenged in court by the D.C. Preservation League.
  • Two National Park Service officials testified virtually; Frank Lands, NPS deputy director of operations, said he was directed to carry out the plan by Greg Wischer, deputy chief of staff at the Interior Department.
  • The work was justified by officials as implementing a 2019 Cultural Landscape(s) Report; Reyes plans a further hearing Tuesday and wants to hear from Wischer.
  • Reyes previously declined to issue restraining orders in the case and expressed reluctance to intervene (e.g., her 'I am not the Lorax' remark).
