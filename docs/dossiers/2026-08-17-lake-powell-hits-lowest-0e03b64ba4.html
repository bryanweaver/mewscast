Consensus Facts

  • Lake Powell, the second-largest reservoir in the United States, dropped to a record-low elevation of 3,519.91 feet on Saturday, breaking the previous record set in April 2023 (ABC News, The Independent).
  • Lake Powell is part of the Colorado River system, which supplies water to roughly 40 million people across seven U.S. states — California, Arizona, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah, Wyoming and Colorado (International Business Times, The Independent).
  • Lake Powell's record low follows Lake Mead — the largest U.S. reservoir — hitting its own record low roughly a week to two weeks earlier (ABC News, The Independent).
  • The combined storage of Lake Powell and Lake Mead is at its lowest level since 1957, before Glen Canyon Dam and Lake Powell existed in their current form (ABC News, International Business Times, The Independent).
  • The reservoir is approximately 30 feet above the elevation at which turbines would cease generating hydroelectric power (International Business Times, The Independent).
  • Jack Schmidt, director of the Center for Colorado River Studies at Utah State University, is cited across outlets characterizing the situation as unprecedented and warning of drastic action (ABC News, International Business Times).
  • Persistent overuse, low snowpack in the Rocky Mountains, and a dry winter are cited as drivers of the decline (International Business Times, The Independent).

The Independent: States the record low as an established fact without emphasizing the provisional caveat
International Business Times: Reports the level as 3,519 feet (rounded) as an established record without the provisional caveat
Distance/threshold to loss of power generation
International Business Times: States turbines stop at 3,490 feet and that a 30-foot further drop would end power generation; cites CNN that this 'scary threshold' could be reached this year without extraordinary measures
The Independent: States the reservoir is about 30 feet above the level where turbines cease generating, and that federal officials warned in April a 'major intervention' is needed to prevent reaching that limit by year's end
