Consensus Facts

  • The U.S. Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) on Thursday, a bill reforming federal environmental permitting and electricity transmission laws.
  • The Senate vote was 62-33.
  • The bill now heads to the House, where its passage is uncertain.
  • President Trump would ultimately need to sign the bill for it to become law.
  • The bill split Democrats, with more Democrats voting against it than for it, while more than a dozen Democrats joined virtually all Republicans to pass it.
  • Democratic opposition largely centered on concerns the bill would weaken environmental protections and favor fossil fuel projects.
  • Sen. Shelley Moore Capito (R-W.Va.) was a key negotiator and supporter of the bill.
  • Sen. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) pushed for an amendment to block data centers from being built on public lands.
  • The bill is designed to speed up both renewable energy and fossil fuel infrastructure projects, including transmission lines.
  • Negotiators announced a breakthrough on the legislation in September 2026.
  • Native American tribes raised objections related to their participation in the permitting process.

Disagreements

Breakdown of Democratic 'yes' votes
CBS News: Fifteen members of the Democratic caucus voted yes; names two GOP 'no' votes (Rounds and Hawley) but does not name Schumer among supporters.

Quantified impact of the bill
CBS News: Does not provide numerical estimates of energy capacity or export changes.

Who brokered the deal
CBS News: Emphasizes Capito and Barrasso (R-Wyo.) as the central figures driving permitting reform.
CNBC: Identifies Sen. Martin Heinrich (D-N.M.) as a lead author who brokered the deal with Sen. Mike Lee (R-Utah), alongside Whitehouse (D-R.I.) and Capito.
