Consensus Facts

  • On Friday, Aug. 7, 2026, a three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit ruled 2-1 that the Trump administration lacks authority to build a new White House ballroom without congressional approval.
  • The project is a roughly 90,000-square-foot ballroom estimated at $400 million, built on the site of the demolished East Wing.
  • Judges Patricia Millett (an Obama appointee) and Bradley Garcia (a Biden appointee) formed the majority; Judge Neomi Rao (a Trump appointee) dissented.
  • The majority wrote that whether a ballroom should be built 'is for Congress to decide and is not a matter for Executive self-help,' and that the ruling has nothing to do with whether the ballroom is desirable as policy.
  • The majority described the president as a 'temporary tenant, not the owner' of the White House, with no constitutionally assigned authority over the property.
  • The panel upheld a lower-court ruling by U.S. District Judge Richard Leon that halted the project.
  • The court paused (stayed) its decision for 14 days to allow the government to seek Supreme Court review.
  • Trump responded on Truth Social that his administration would 'immediately' appeal to the Supreme Court, calling the ruling 'horrendous, politically motivated, and unlawful.'
  • Trump characterized the ballroom project as tied to national security, and the ruling permits below-ground construction (including a bunker/security infrastructure) to continue.
  • The lawsuit was brought by the National Trust for Historic Preservation, a privately funded nonprofit, whose president and CEO Brent Leggs praised the ruling.
  • The East Wing was demolished in October 2025 and construction began without congressional approval.
  • In her dissent, Rao argued the National Trust lacked standing to sue and that the president likely had authority to build the ballroom.
